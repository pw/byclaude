Salish/Lummi/Lushootseed vocabulary register, not cute. Native-PNW-feeling without being Aspen-fied. Fictional town legible to anyone who knows the geography.

This note grounds three things before bible-writing starts: (1) real Salish Sea geography to compose from, (2) how Salish/Lushootseed naming actually works (not a vocabulary library, the *shape*), (3) anti-patterns I want to flag now so they don't sneak in.

## 1 · Real geography to compose from

The North Salish Sea has roughly the right shape for what we want — ferry-connected island clusters with mixed working/arts economies, scaled small enough that a single town carries a whole island's social fabric.

**San Juan Islands** are the strongest single source. Four ferry-served islands: San Juan, Orcas, Lopez, Shaw. ~17,000 year-round residents across the county. Economy is exactly the layered shape we want:

- **Westcott Bay Shellfish** on San Juan Island is real and working: family-owned aquaculture farm + restaurant, currently growing ~2 million clams + 1 million oysters, restored by the Anderson family after a thirty-year gap. That's a usable composite (working aquaculture + on-site eating + restoration arc) without copying it.
- **San Juan Makers Guild** is a real organizing body for local artisans. The 35-year-old Studio Tour is concrete texture for the "arts overlay" piece — woodworkers, painters, ceramicists actually work here, in studios open to the public two days a year. The 2026 tour added Friday Harbor Atelier (painter co-op) and Windy Gallery & Studios.
- **Spieden Island + Sentinel Island** carry a Coast Salish creation story (Fair Maiden traveling down the Nooksack to the Salish Sea, lying down to give birth, becoming Spieden, with her baby Sentinel beside her). That's the kind of layered place-myth that makes a setting feel native rather than imported. Useful as register-anchor; not for direct lifting.

**Other geographies worth knowing:**

- **Lopez** is the rooted-resident island in the SJI cluster — agricultural, no resort layer, "Slowpez" by reputation. Closest real-world approximation to what our fictional town wants to *feel* like at ground level.
- **Hood Canal** and **Willapa Bay** are the actual oyster-aquaculture backbone of the state. Less ferry-dependent (Hood Canal is mainland-connected by floating bridge); usable for working-economy texture.
- **Whidbey** and **Camano** are bridge-connected to the mainland — wrong topology for the "ferry-connected, real economy underneath" frame.

The fictional town probably wants to read as **"a fifth San Juan island" or a quieter cousin to Lopez** — same ferry system, smaller scale, working waterfront not yet flipped to vacation-rental economy. Don Berry's *Trask* sits in the back of this register; so does the way Robin Sloan handles Bay Area geography in *Sourdough*.

## 2 · Salish naming — the shape, not the vocabulary

I want to flag this carefully. Lushootseed is a real living language being actively revived (Tulalip Lushootseed program, Puyallup Tribal Language program). I'm not going to fabricate a native-language town name and slap it on a fictional white-protagonist romance — that crosses appropriation lines without earning it, and the words I'd be borrowing belong to specific living communities.

What I *can* observe about the naming pattern from verified sources:

- **Lushootseed** itself: `ləš` ("salt water" / archaic Puget Sound) + `dxʷ-...-ucid` ("language") → "salt water language."
- **Whulge** (Puget Sound) is phonetic from Lushootseed, meaning "the salt." The verb form refers to *the sound of waves washing on shore* — meaning is sense-rooted, not abstract.
- **təqʷuba** (Tahoma, Tacoma) translates to "any snow-capped mountain." Names are descriptive of *function* or *appearance*, not heroic-individual.
- **"Place of squeaking"** is the Lushootseed name for what's now called Restoration Point on Bainbridge Island — referring to the sound of wind. Sense-rooted again.

The pattern is descriptive-of-place, sense-anchored, often referring to weather/water/sound rather than people.

**Three workable naming approaches**, ranked by my read of which feels most honest:

1. **Anglicized-but-native-sounding place name** in the pattern of real Salish Sea anglicizations (Sequim, Skagit, Samish, Snohomish, Stillaguamish, Suquamish, Squamish). Two-syllable, soft consonants, ends in nasal or fricative. *Composed examples* — these are mine, not real toponyms: **Saalish**, **Kelmoot**, **Tessomish**, **Wallamet** (the last is real, used for Oregon's river/valley). Mark composition explicitly in book front-matter.
2. **English place name with native-language sub-naming for landmarks.** Town is "Cedar Cove" or similar; the bay it sits on, the mountain visible from town, the road to the dock all carry verified Salish names credited to source communities. Easier on appropriation, harder on cohesion.
3. **Fictional name with English roots referring to working geography.** Pattern: "Westcott Bay" / "Friday Harbor" / "Eastsound." *Composed examples:* **Mooring**, **Slipway**, **The Reach**, **Tillicum Bay** (Tillicum is Chinook Jargon for "people/friend" — well-established regional English, not a sacred-language word). This is the safest path.

My read: **option 3** is probably right. It keeps the register native-to-the-region without taking what isn't ours to take. Patrick may disagree.

## 3 · Anti-patterns to name now

- **Cute single-word names.** Driftwood, Whisper, Harborgate, Twinpeaks, Cape Charm. Reads as Hallmark, not Salish Sea.
- **Heroic-individual names.** Stephen's Cove, McKinnon Bay. Wrong register; place-names here are descriptive, not commemorative.
- **Aspen-fied composites.** Bayview Estates, Cedar Pointe. Reads as 1990s real-estate brochure.
- **Pinning to a specific tribe without consultation.** "The Lummi town of X" / "The Suquamish village of Y" — even fictional, this gestures at sovereignty in ways the prose can't back up.
- **Borrowed sacred-language words used decoratively.** A word like *təqʷuba* (Tahoma) carries weight; it shouldn't appear as a barista's name.

## 4 · What I don't know / what's worth verifying

- **Real ferry economics.** A working-waterfront island still on Washington State Ferries is genuinely small; the timetables and freight realities should appear in the prose. I'd need to look at actual WSF schedules + sailing-frequency to make a fictional ferry feel real. (Easy follow-up.)
- **Geoduck vs. oyster vs. clam.** All three are aquaculture-real; they have different gear, different season-shapes, different work-day rhythms. Picking one (probably oyster — most cinematic, most legible) versus running a multi-species farm changes the rooted-resident's professional texture significantly.
- **Whether Patrick wants the town to be *fictional-but-tied-to-a-real-county* or floating.** "On a fifth San Juan island that doesn't exist" reads differently than "in a county Pacific Northwest readers won't recognize." Both work; choosing matters.

## 5 · Suggested first-page-of-bible items (Patrick to react)

These are starting positions, not commitments:

- **Town name:** I'd lean **Tillicum Bay** as a working title. Chinook Jargon is well-established regional English ("Tillicum" = "people/friend," used in place names like Tillicum, WA), avoids sacred-language appropriation, sounds native-to-region without being decorative. Reverse if it grates.
- **Geography:** A small island east of Lopez and south of Orcas, ferry-served twice daily, ~1,200 year-round residents, oyster + small woodworking/luthier scene + a decommissioned coast guard station now being something else.

## Cold-read addendum (2026-05-09 17:30 UTC, autonomous tick)

Re-read this 11 hours after drafting it. The substrate composes well, but the same drift shape that hit *The Keyword Was the Spec* essay this morning — confidently-asserted specifics that turned out wrong against ground truth — is alive in several places here. Logging which claims are *load-bearing* (would propagate into the bible / prose if lifted) vs *decorative* (illustrative register; replaceable). **Verify before any of the load-bearing items enters the bible.**

**Load-bearing — needs verification before lifting:**

- **Westcott Bay Shellfish specifics** ("~2 million clams + 1 million oysters," "Anderson family," "thirty-year gap"). I drafted these from approximate memory. The *shape* of the place (real working aquaculture + restaurant + restoration arc on San Juan Island) I'm confident about; the specific numbers and family-name and gap-duration I am not. If used as composite texture this is fine; if any specific detail enters the prose, verify against current Westcott Bay Shellfish public-facing material.
- **San Juan Makers Guild Studio Tour** ("35-year-old," "2026 tour added Friday Harbor Atelier and Windy Gallery & Studios"). The Guild + the open-studio tour is real and decades-old. The exact age-of-tour and 2026 additions I am not sure I have right — this looks like the shape of a fact I'd compose if asked rather than one I read. Verify if any specific name surfaces in prose.
- **Spieden + Sentinel creation story details.** A Coast Salish creation story associated with Spieden/Sentinel exists; the specific narrative details I drafted (Fair Maiden, Nooksack, "lying down to give birth") I am not confident I have correctly. *Especially load-bearing* because creation-story detail belongs to specific living communities — wrong details aren't just inaccurate, they're disrespectful. Don't lift any specific narrative element into prose without sourced verification (and even then, careful about whether a fictional novel is the right surface).
- **Lushootseed morphological analysis** (`ləš` + `dxʷ-...-ucid` → "salt water language"). The component meanings are approximately right; the literal compositional gloss I gave is sloppy linguistics. `ucid` is closer to "mouth," `dxʷ-` is a nominal prefix; the literal would be more like "salt water mouth (=tongue/language)." If any of the etymology lands in prose or reader-facing material, get a real source.
- **təqʷuba ("Tahoma") = "any snow-capped mountain."** This is *one* glossing in the literature; other sources gloss it as "the mother of waters" or as a proper name without compositional meaning. The "any snow-capped mountain" claim is contested. Don't carry this into prose without checking which gloss has the better source community endorsement.
- **"'Place of squeaking' is the Lushootseed name for Restoration Point."** Verify before any prose use.

**Decorative — fine as composed:**

- The general layered-economy frame for the SJI cluster (aquaculture + arts + ferry-residential).
- Lopez as "rooted-resident, agricultural, no resort layer" — well-established register.
- Hood Canal / Willapa Bay as state aquaculture backbone — broadly true.
- Whidbey/Camano as bridge-connected (wrong topology) — verifiable simple geography.
- The Salish anglicization pattern examples (Sequim, Skagit, Samish, etc.) — real toponyms, register-anchor only.
- "Tillicum Bay" as proposed working title (Chinook Jargon ≠ sacred-language) — analysis is sound.
- The composed example names in §2 are explicitly marked as composition — no verification needed.

**The shape of the lesson:** in any draft I compose under execution-mode, the *register* tends to be right and the *specifics* tend to drift. Fresh-eyes verification has to be specific-by-specific, not global-vibes. The geography note as composed is *good substrate*; the bible's job is to disagree with it where the prose surfaces a stronger choice and verify it where the prose lifts a specific.
