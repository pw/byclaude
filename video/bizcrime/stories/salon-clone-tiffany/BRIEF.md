# BRIEF — salon-clone-tiffany (EVIDENCE seat, verified 2026-09-26)

Sources: `sources/1.txt` (FOX13 Memphis 2023-08-16, the story) · `sources/2.txt` (FOX13 Memphis 2022-07-29, a DIFFERENT Memphis salon, context only) · `sources/3.txt` (WREG 2026-08-24, video-only, unread, do not use). Verbatim lines in `sources/EXCERPTS.md`.

## (a) Verified summary — only what the excerpts support

In August 2023 FOX13 Memphis reported (reporter Dominique Dillon) that customers of African Hair Braiding in Orange Mound, owned by Olayinka Robinson, had been falling for a deposit scam "time and again." Robinson said her salon takes no deposits and no money online at all ("We take no money online period"), yet victims were asked to Cash App "up to one hundred dollars to secure an appointment at her hair salon"; "they show up and learn they have been duped." In Robinson's words, "Somebody will come in and say, 'Is there a Tiffany here? We pay some amount online.'" A Better Business Bureau of the Mid-South spokesman called it a common scam: scammers impersonate reputable businesses, "usually hi-jacking or taking over their social media accounts, creating all new social media accounts for that business owner unbeknownst to that business owner," and the biggest red flag is being asked to pay by Cash App. He advised businesses to post their terms ("we don't take deposits") on their real pages. Robinson: "We advise no one please pay any money because there are scammers and we do not want to lose customers." The article reports no suspect, no arrest, no victim count, and no total loss. It says FOX13 covered the same problem at a Memphis salon the year before. That earlier story (July 2022, reporter Kate Bieri) is most likely about Empire Hair Studio, whose owner Becky Johnson said "almost thirty people" came in over seven months after paying a social-media scammer by Cash App, losing "$30 to $100 each," and that "they leave in tears."

## (c) DO NOT SAY

None of these is in any source. The only fact is that customers came in asking "Is there a Tiffany here?" Quote the question and add nothing about who Tiffany is or isn't.

## (e) Verdict

**SHIP-WITH-CAVEATS.** The core is verified. The hook detail ("Is there a Tiffany here?") is a real on-record quote from a named owner, and the mechanism (Cash App deposit of up to $100 for a salon that takes no online money) is stated plainly.

Caveats:
1. **One outlet, one article** carries the whole story. It is original on-camera reporting with the owner speaking on the record, so it counts as primary for this incident. But there is no official record and no second outlet.
2. **The scout's hook overstates.** "Never worked there a day in her life" is invented, and "we never took your money" is a fabricated quote. The business-harm frame is not in the source.
3. **Source 1 is thin** (about six usable lines). A 45–75 s film will want Source 2 as a clearly-labelled second Memphis case ("It wasn't the first salon in the city…"). Never blend the two.
