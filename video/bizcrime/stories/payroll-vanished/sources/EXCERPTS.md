## 1 — DOJ sentencing release (PRIMARY)
https://www.justice.gov/usao-ndny/pr/valuewise-ceo-michael-mann-sentenced-144-months-prison-100-million-fraud

**Who / role**
> "Michael T. Mann, age 51, was sentenced today to 144 months in prison for running a complex scheme that caused more than $100 million in losses to banks, financing companies, and other businesses, and also involved his misappropriation of millions of dollars entrusted to payroll companies that he owned."

> "Mann operated ValueWise Corporation, based in Clifton Park, New York, as well as subsidiary companies including MyPayrollHR.com LLC."

> "A co-conspirator, former Optum employee Luke E. Steiner, age 33, of Minneapolis, Minnesota, pled guilty in February 2020 to conspiring with Mann to defraud two financing companies out of millions of dollars."

**When / what (the scheme)**
> "He admitted that from 2013 to September 2019, he engaged in a fraudulent scheme to deceive banks and financing companies into loaning his companies tens of millions of dollars. Because Mann could not repay the loans with legitimate business revenues, he expanded the fraud, by stealing and diverting millions of dollars that were entrusted to his payroll companies, and engaging in the daily kiting of millions of dollars among bank accounts he controlled."

> "Mann created fake invoices reflecting the fictitious debt and assigned them to the financing companies as collateral for the loans."

> "Mann fraudulently obtained a line of credit from several Capital Region banks; the line of credit had grown to $42 million by August 2019."

**The payroll mechanism**
> "Mann misappropriated payroll monies, entrusted to MyPayrollHR and other payroll companies he owned, by changing the instructions for digital Automated Clearing House (“ACH”) files that were supposed to transmit payroll from customers (employers) to the employees of the customers."

> "Mann changed the instructions inside digital ACH files provided to Cachet, in order to divert payroll funds into accounts that he controlled at Pioneer Bank."

**The collapse**
> "Mann’s scheme collapsed in late August and early September 2019, when one of his banks froze his accounts, setting off a chain of events that left his payroll companies unable to process payroll and tax payments for hundreds of small business customers nationwide."

> "When Pioneer Bank froze Mann’s corporate accounts on or about August 30, 2019, it froze the payroll funds in those accounts, and caused several thousand people across the country to not receive a payroll payment."

**Outcome**
> "On August 12, 2020, Mann pled guilty to one count of conspiracy to commit wire fraud, one count of aggravated identity theft, nine counts of bank fraud, and one count of filing a false tax return."

> "Senior United States District Judge Lawrence E. Kahn also ordered Mann to serve 3 years of post-imprisonment supervised release, pay restitution to victims in the total amount of $101,038,793.31, and to forfeit assets already seized by the Government, including $14,522,474.90 contained in bank accounts and 30,000 common shares of Pioneer Bancorp Inc."

**Harm to small businesses (official quotes)**
> "He also stole the paychecks of thousands of hard-working people, and the tax payments of hundreds of small businesses, across the country.  Mann caused immense stress for small business owners and fear among employees living paycheck to paycheck, and he justly deserves the term of imprisonment imposed today."
— Acting U.S. Attorney Antoinette T. Bacon

> "For years, instead of growing a legitimate business in Clifton Park, he grew a fraudulent scheme."
— Acting U.S. Attorney Antoinette T. Bacon

> "Not only were thousands of employees impacted, but some small business owners lost their livelihoods when they went out of business due to Mann’s criminal scheme."
— FBI SAC Janeen DiGuiseppi

## 2 — DOJ charging release (PRIMARY)
https://www.justice.gov/usao-ndny/pr/michael-mann-charged-bank-fraud

> "Michael T. Mann, age 49, of Saratoga County, New York, appeared in federal court today on a criminal complaint charging him with committing a $70 million bank fraud."

> "Mann began the fraudulent scheme in 2010 or 2011."

> "Mann operates MyPayrollHr, based in Clifton Park, New York."

> "Until this month, MyPayrollHR processed payroll and tax payments for approximately 1,000 small-business clients located across the country."

> "On September 5, 2019, MyPayrollHR suddenly ceased operations after Mann’s banks froze his accounts, suspecting him of fraud.  This account freeze affected MyPayrollHR’s clients because, as part of the fraudulent scheme, Mann diverted clients’ payroll payments to a bank account he controlled."

> "He created companies that had no purpose other than to be used in the fraud; fraudulently represented to banks and financing companies that his fake businesses had certain receivables that they did not have; and obtained loans and lines of credit by borrowing against these non-existent receivables."

(Charging-stage caveat, same release: "The charges in the complaint are merely accusations. The defendant is presumed innocent unless and until proven guilty." — superseded by the Aug 12, 2020 guilty plea in source 1.)

## 5 — KrebsOnSecurity, Sept 11, 2019
https://krebsonsecurity.com/2019/09/ny-payroll-company-vanishes-with-35-million/

> "MyPayrollHR, a now defunct cloud-based payroll processing firm based in upstate New York, abruptly ceased operations this past week after stiffing employees at thousands of companies."

> "Clifton Park, NY-based MyPayrollHR — a subsidiary of ValueWise Corp. — disclosed last week in a rather unceremonious message to some 4,000 clients that it would be shutting its virtual doors and that companies which relied upon it to process payroll payments should kindly look elsewhere for such services going forward."

> "This communique came after employees at companies that depend on MyPayrollHR to receive direct deposits of their bi-weekly payroll payments discovered their bank accounts were instead debited for the amounts they would normally expect to accrue in a given pay period."

> "To make matters worse, many of those employees found their accounts had been dinged for two payroll periods — a month’s worth of wages — leaving their bank accounts dangerously in the red."

> "But according to Cachet, something odd happened with the instructions file MyPayrollHR submitted on the afternoon of Wednesday, Sept. 4 that had never before transpired: MyPayrollHR requested that all of its clients’ payroll dollars be sent not to Cachet’s holding account but instead to an account at Pioneer Savings Bank that was operated and controlled by MyPayrollHR."

> "The total amount of this mass payroll deposit was approximately $26 million."

> "Slavkin said Cachet managed to reach the CEO of MyPayrollHR — Michael T. Mann — via phone on the evening of Sept. 4, and that Mann said he would would call back in a few minutes. According to Slavkin, Mann never returned the call."

> "But according to Slavkin, that initial reversal request was improperly formatted, and so Cachet soon after submitted a correctly coded reversal request."

> "But Slavkin said Cachet ultimately decided to cancel the previous payment reversals, leaving Cachet on the hook for $26 million."

> "the now defunct New York company also stiffed National Payment Corporation (NatPay) — the Florida-based firm which handles tax withholdings for MyPayrollHR clients — to the tune of more than $9 million."

**On-record business-owner victim**
> "Dan L’Abbe, CEO of the San Francisco-based consultancy Granite Solutions Groupe, said the mix-up has been massively disruptive for his 250 employees."

> "“This caused a lot of chaos for employers, but employees were the ones really affected,” L’Abbe said. “This is all very unusual because we don’t even have the ability to take money out of our employee accounts.”"

> "Several erstwhile MyPayrollHR employees reached via LinkedIn said none of them had seen or heard from Mr. Mann in days."

## 7 — Times Union, Aug 4, 2021 (sentencing day)
https://www.timesunion.com/business/article/Victims-of-MyPayrollHR-collapse-reveal-pain-and-16360790.php

> "Mann's decadelong scheme to defraud his lenders so that he could buy companies like MyPayrollHR and fund other business ventures, including a physical therapy chain and a high school basketball academy, was uncovered during Labor Day weekend in 2019."

> "Although Mann's bankers didn't catch on to what Mann was doing until 2019, he had been repeatedly warned by the California company that helped to move his clients' payroll through the banking system to stop the practice. Mann never did, and the company, Cachet Financial Services, was forced to file for bankruptcy."

**Vivid detail — the diner (from the government's sentencing memo, as reported)**
> "In their sentencing memo, federal prosecutors lead with the story of Bonnie Rosengrant, the owner of the Hometown Diner, a popular eatery in Rindge, N.H., near Franklin Pierce University."

> "Rosengrant was sitting at the bedside of her critically ill mother, holding her hand, during the first week in September 2019 when she received a call from her manager that none of the diner's roughly two dozen employees had been paid that week — despite the fact that the funds had already been pulled from the diner's bank account."

> "Alarmed, Rosengrant left her mother's bedside and stepped into a financial mess so horrible that she and her husband were forced to use $30,000 of their life savings to pay their employees their lost wages and cover their payroll taxes."

> "By the following June, with the COVID-19 pandemic having ravaged the restaurant industry, Rosengrant opted to retire and closed the diner."

> ""Before September 2019, the name Michael Mann meant nothing to Ms. Rosengrant, and she never imagined that her payroll processor would steal from her and her hard-working employees.""  (prosecutors, per TU)

> ""Michael Mann, I want you to know this is what being violated will do to you," Rosengrant wrote. "You cannot shut it off and it will not go away.""  (victim impact statement)

**The "angry mob" (scout's anecdote) — an UNNAMED company's statement cited by prosecutors**
> "A staffing company in Massachusetts that used MyPayrollHR said that employees initially assumed the company had stolen their paychecks, leading to an angry mob assembling in the company's lobby for days demanding justice."

> ""We experienced threats of violence against the company broadly as well as against individual managers, including death threats, during this time, necessitating the hiring of a police officer to guard our offices," an unidentified company executive wrote in another statement cited by prosecutors."

**Other**
> "Even animals suffered the consequences, the manager of an animal shelter in Wisconsin that used MyPayrollHR and lost $18,000 said in their statement to the court."

> "He says that although Mann ripped off his lenders to the tune of $100 million, he only paid himself an annual salary of $200,000, dating back to before he began the fraud in 2013, and never gave himself a raise."  (defense attorney Michael Koenig's position)
