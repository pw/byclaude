# BRIEF — google-pin-moved (evidence seat, 2026-09-26)

Sources: `sources/1.txt`–`5.txt`, verbatim lines in `sources/EXCERPTS.md`. [1] = Sherwood News 2024-07-15 (the ONLY source for the Insulation Pros case). [2] SEL, [3] Near Media, [4] BrightLocal = the general pin-moving problem. [5] SEO This Week = the later "fix" claim (weak).

## (a) Verified summary
In mid-2024, a Kansas City web-design and SEO consultant went to visit his client, Insulation Pros, an insulation contractor based in Independence, Missouri. Google Maps showed a 20-minute drive now taking 45, and partway there he realized it was sending him the wrong way, north toward Smithville, where the business's pin had been moved [1]. The listing still showed the Independence address, but "directions" led somewhere else [1]. The company already knew: some of its own drivers had ended up in the wrong place looking for their home base. "We had no notification, no warning," the consultant said [1]. They first assumed a glitch. Then they found "a number of pending edits to the location moving the business’s map pin even farther away" [1]. Drivers went to the wrong place for tools and equipment, which cost the company money. It "no longer showed up in Independence," and the next month it saw "an unseasonably large decrease in inquiries" [1]. Nobody has established who moved the pin. The consultant suspects rivals ("I can't say that this is actually what happened... It’s very cutthroat") [1]. Local-search writers described anyone being able to drag a business's pin through Google's public "Suggest an edit," with Google not notifying the owner [2][4]. In a statement, Google said it was "currently investigating" and taking action on "abusive accounts" [1]. Insulation Pros declined to comment [1].

## (c) DO NOT SAY
- **"On purpose" / "sabotage" as fact (the scout's title).** Not established. [1] asks it as a question in the headline. It says "impossible to say definitively who or what" (about Miranda), and Jackson says "I can't say that this is actually what happened." The owners themselves "presumed … a glitch." The pending edits pushing the pin farther are suggestive, not proof. The allowed wording is attributed and hedged: "their consultant suspects a competitor", "SEO writers call it a scam competitors use" [2][3]. No title may assert intent.
- **"Someone used 'Suggest an edit' to move Insulation Pros' pin."** [1] never names the feature for this case. It says only "a number of pending edits to the location." "Suggest an edit" is the general mechanism in [2][3][4]. Present it as how the trick works in general, not as a documented fact about this listing.
- **"More than 20 miles away."** No source gives any distance for Independence→Smithville. "20" is the scout conflating the 20-minute drive time. The only sourced facts are direction: Smithville is "due north of Kansas City" and Independence is "east of Kansas City." For a mileage figure, the sourced one belongs to a DIFFERENT business: 1st Class Plumbing, moved 43 miles, Rochester→Red Wing MN [3].
- **"Delivery drivers."** They were the company's OWN drivers, looking for home base and going "to pick up the tools and equipment" [1]. Nothing says delivery drivers.
- **"Customers were routed to the wrong location."** Not documented for this case. Jackson says only "I don't think a customer would have known." A general claim is allowed if attributed: "directions … taking customers to the wrong place" [3].
- **"Ranking collapsed" / "vanished from its neighborhood" / "disappeared from the map."** Use the source's words: "SEO ranking also fell" and "We no longer showed up in Independence." "Dropped off the map" is Near Media's line about the Rochester plumber, not about Insulation Pros.
- **Any dollar loss figure.** [1] says only "lose money through inefficiencies." No amount exists.
- **Size of the drop in inquiries.** Only "an unseasonably large decrease in inquiries," with no number.
- **"Google never notified the business" as a fact about Google.** Keep it Jackson's claim: "We had no notification, no warning." The general claim "Google doesn’t notify the owners" is SEL/BrightLocal's claim; attribute it.
- **Exact date.** [1] says only "About a month ago" relative to 2024-07-15. "In 2024" or "summer 2024" is fine. "June 14" or any specific day is not.
- **That the pin was fixed, or any outcome for Insulation Pros.** No source reports a resolution.
- **"Google fixed it in December 2024" / "Google announced a patch."** [5] contradicts itself: it says Google "has not posted any official announcement" and later says "In December 2024, Google announced." Its basis is a Jan 10, 2025 tweet by Ben Fisher, not a Google statement. BrightLocal says "fixed … reported in January 2025." The most you can say is "In early 2025, local-search experts reported the loophole had been closed." Better: leave it out.
- **"Google trusts random people more than business owners" as narrator fact.** It is a quote from Miranda and Darren Shaw. Attribute it ("one search expert put it this way").
- **"Nobody hacked anything."** Mostly fine as framing, but no source investigated the edit's origin. Prefer "no password was stolen; the edit came through the public map."
- **The scout's hook "the business quietly vanished from its own neighborhood on the map everyone uses to find it."** It overstates. The business still appeared in search. It stopped ranking in Independence, and directions went to the wrong place. "Vanished" is not supported.
- **The Insulation Pros owner's feelings, words or reactions.** The company declined to comment, and everything is relayed through Jackson. No owner quotes and no interior states.

- **Insulation Pros**: named by [1], so it MAY be named. It is the victim business, and the company declined comment. My recommendation is to name it once at most, or use "an insulation contractor in Independence, Missouri." The story doesn't need the name, and there is no villain to pair it with.

## (e) Verdict
**SHIP-WITH-CAVEATS.** The vivid spine is literally supported by an on-record interview in [1]. It includes the 20→45-minute drive, the turn north toward Smithville, the correct address with a wrong pin, the company's own drivers lost looking for their home base, and more pending edits pushing the pin "even farther away." The system villain (anyone can edit, the owner isn't told) is corroborated by three independent trade sources and Google's own "investigating" statement. The caveats:
1. The case itself is single-outlet. Sherwood's is original reporting, not a re-report, and no court or agency record exists.
2. The business declined comment, so everything arrives through its consultant.
3. The scout's title claim "On Purpose" is **not established** and must become a question or an attributed suspicion.
4. "20+ miles", "delivery drivers", "customers routed" and "Suggest an edit (for this listing)" are unsupported and must be dropped or reattributed as described above.
It is not a KILL because the villain is the design of the system, not a private misfortune, and the detail that makes it a video is sourced. It would become a KILL if the writer needs intent asserted to make the film work.
