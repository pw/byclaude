# BRIEF: refund-the-complainers (verified by the evidence seat, 2026-09-26)

Sources are in `sources/1.txt`–`7.txt`. Verbatim lines are in `sources/EXCERPTS.md`. The primary spine is the **sealed criminal complaint (source 2)** plus the **DOJ SDNY release (source 1)**. The outcome comes from the **PACER docket (source 5)**, corroborated by the Laguna Beach Independent (source 4).

## (a) Verified summary

According to a federal criminal complaint, a man named Mark Mushkin mailed false invoices for copier toner from January 2011 to June 2017. They went out under the name of a company he ran called IT Tech Products, and the targets were small businesses around the country, mostly law firms. The invoices "falsely stated shipment dates for copier toner". They typically charged $419 or $429 per unit. The businesses had never ordered any toner.

The company's registered address included a suite (#202) that the building's manager says does not exist. Payments went to P.O. Box 12017 in Costa Mesa, California. A post-office camera recorded a man checking that box nine times in March and April 2011, and the postal inspector identified him as Mushkin.

The bank account received approximately **1,919 checks from 878 companies, totaling approximately $3,334,238**. Across the six and a half years, the complaint found that only **96 units of toner were ever bought**, all of them in 2011 from one Phoenix company.

Prosecutors said that when a business called to complain after it had paid, "generally MUSHKIN or a representative of IT Tech Products would send a refund. This practice allowed MUSHKIN to continue operating the scheme." The record shows **41 refund checks to 36 companies (about $128,370)**. That is a small fraction of the 878 companies that paid.

Examples from the complaint:
- One White Plains law firm questioned an $872 invoice in 2011 and received a copy stamped "VOID - Cancelled." In 2017 it got another invoice, and this time it went to law enforcement.
- Another firm paid $1,690, then found out its toner was already free under its copier contract.
- A Manhattan firm paid three invoices in 2016. It left several messages, and a man calling himself "Bob Stevens" refunded it.

Mushkin, 52, of Laguna Beach, was arrested and charged with one count of mail fraud on June 13, 2018, and indicted in August 2018. The docket records a "Change of Not Guilty Plea" on April 5, 2019, and the *Laguna Beach Independent* reported that he "pleaded guilty ... to felony mail fraud." Judgment and an Order for Forfeiture of Property were entered **September 24, 2019**. **The sentence is not publicly available** (the judgment is behind PACER).

## (b) Hook candidates (each tied to an excerpt)

1. **"If you called to complain after you paid, the fake toner company would usually just send your money back. Prosecutors say that is how it kept going for six years."**
   - Source 2, ¶17c: "In the event that a Victim Business called ... to complain about the invoice after having made payment, generally MUSHKIN or a representative of IT Tech Products would send a refund. This practice allowed MUSHKIN to continue operating the scheme."
   - "Six years" is Jan 2011 to Jun 2017 (¶1). Safer wording is "from 2011 to 2017".
2. **"In six years, the company bought 96 units of toner, all of them in 2011. Its bank account took in 1,919 checks for toner."**
   - Source 2, ¶16: "only 96 units of copier toner were purchased ... All 96 units ... in 2011."
   - Source 2, ¶13c: "approximately 1,919 checks from 878 companies totaling approximately $3,334,238."
3. **"One law firm had a toner invoice come back stamped 'VOID.' In 2017, the same company billed it again."**
   - Source 2, ¶9d: "marked 'VOID - Cancelled October 29, 2011.'"
   - Source 2, ¶9e: "On or about February 6, 2017, Law Firm-1 received another invoice".
   - Strictly, that gap is 5 years 3 months (Nov 2011 to Feb 2017). Say "years later" or "in 2017", not "six years later".

## (c) DO NOT SAY

- **"refunded you immediately"** (scout's `why_it_hooks`). Not supported. Law Firm-3 paid invoices in May, June and July 2016. After the fourth invoice (August 5) it "called ... and left several messages", and its refund checks are dated September 8 and 15, 2016. The record says "generally ... would send a refund", which does not say quickly.
- **"If you complained, he refunded you" as a universal ("every complainer got a refund").** The record says "generally". There were 342 complaints but refund checks went to only 36 companies (plus "some" certified-check refunds). Businesses that complained *before* paying got a voided invoice, not a refund (Law Firm-1).
- **"the perfect trick", or anything claiming the refunds *caused* him to go undetected as a proven fact.** Say it as "prosecutors said / the complaint says" ("This practice allowed MUSHKIN to continue operating the scheme"). It is the government's characterization.
- **"878 small businesses".** The primary record says "878 companies" (source 2). The DOJ release says "Over 800 businesses". "Small businesses" as a label for all 878 comes from Westfair (secondary). Use "878 companies", or "more than 800 businesses, mostly law firms".
- **"For six years", stated as exact.** The DOJ release says "Over more than five years" and "Between about 2011 and 2017". The complaint says January 2011 to June 2017. Use "from 2011 to 2017".
- **"He deposited 1,919 checks" as an admitted fact.** These are the complaint's figures ("approximately"). The guilty plea is to mail fraud, but the plea allocution and judgment are not public, so we don't know what figures he admitted. Attribute them: "according to prosecutors / the complaint".
- **Anything about his sentence: prison, length, restitution amount, forfeiture amount.** Unknown. The docket shows only the titles "Judgment" and "Order for Forfeiture of Property" (9/24/2019) and a supervised-release transfer (12/2023). A BOP locator record shows a "MARK MUSHKIN", age 60, released 05/28/2021. That fits, but the identity is inferred from name and age only, so do not state that he went to prison.
- **"convicted after trial".** He pleaded guilty. There was no trial.
- **Westfair's "profit of at least 95 percent" / "netted nearly $3.2 million".** This is the journalist's own arithmetic, not the complaint's. It counts only $8,953 of mailing costs (the Additional Bank Accounts' UPS+USPS). It leaves out the main account's $3,417 UPS + $1,611 USPS, and it assumes no other expenses. Do not use it.
- **"at least three New York law firms called law enforcement"** (Laguna Beach Independent). The complaint says only Law Firm-1 "contacted a law enforcement officer". The others appear through "reports of conversations between law enforcement and an employee".
- **"In June 2014"** (Laguna Beach Independent, Law Firm-2's first invoice). The complaint says "In or around July 2014 ... an invoice dated July 10, 2014".
- **Westfair's "$858" / "$2,514" given as invoice totals.** Those are the toner lines. The totals with $14 shipping were $872 and $2,528 (complaint). If you give a figure, give the complaint's total.
- **"Bob Stevens was Mushkin"**, or presenting "Bob Stevens" as a real separate person. The complaint only says a man "identified himself as Bob Stevens". It does not say who he was. Phrase it as "a man calling himself Bob Stevens".
- **Any dialogue beyond the complaint's paraphrase.** The recorded 2017 call is described only "in substance and in part" ("the invoice was voided and the order was cancelled before any toner was shipped out"). Do not write quoted speech for it.
- **"Xerox" in the VO.** It is a real brand quoted from one invoice line ("TONER FOR XEROX WORKCENTRE 5632"), and Xerox did nothing wrong. Say "copier toner".
- **"Mark Timothy Gallery" / "photographer".** These appear only in the Laguna Beach Independent (secondary). The gallery is a separate, legitimate business that is not in the official record. Do not name it. The "photographer" descriptor is single-source, so avoid it.
- **"FTC action" or other agencies prosecuting.** The DOJ release only "thanked the Federal Trade Commission for their assistance". Complaints were filed with the BBB, the FTC and USPIS. There is no FTC case.
- **DailyVoice article.** It was BLOCKED (Cloudflare on every tier, no archive). Nothing from it is verified.

## (e) Verdict

**SHIP-WITH-CAVEATS.** The story rests on two primary sources (the sworn complaint and the DOJ release), with the outcome confirmed on the PACER docket. The villain is a charged-and-pled perpetrator, not someone's private misfortune. The vivid details all hold up in the complaint: the VOID-stamped invoice, the P.O. box on camera, 96 units of toner against 1,919 checks, and the firm whose toner was already free.

The caveats:
1. The hook must drop "immediately" and "everyone". The refunds were "generally" given to post-payment callers, and they reached only 36 companies.
2. The outcome line is **"He pleaded guilty to mail fraud"** (2019), with no sentence stated.
3. The dollar and count figures stay attributed to prosecutors / the complaint.
