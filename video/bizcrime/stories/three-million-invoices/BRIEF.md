# Verified brief — "three-million-invoices" (U.S. v. Robert W. Lederhilger III, SDNY 23 Cr. 116)

Evidence seat, 2026-09-26. Every claim below traces to `sources/EXCERPTS.md` ([n] = source number).

## (a) Verified summary

Beginning in 2015, Robert W. Lederhilger III, of Bradenton, Florida, ran companies that "purported to offer webhosting services" [2]. He mailed, and caused others to mail, mailers that "falsely appeared to their recipients—primarily small businesses that already had websites—to be invoices for purported web hosting services," typically listing "$180 as the amount due" [1]. The mailers carried "a due date, the amount due, a description of the services provided, i.e. 'Yearly Web Hosting,' a phone number for billing inquiries" [3]; checks went to a commercial mailbox on Park Avenue South in Manhattan [3]. He also sold a "Reseller Program" whose participants mailed the same kind of pieces under their own company names and paid him about 44% of each payment [3]. Victims "whose websites were hosted by other providers, received nothing from the defendant except another 'invoice,' a year later, asking for more money" [1]. A Connecticut consumer agency publicly warned businesses about "Web Host Agents" invoices in August 2019 [5]. He was arrested March 8, 2023 [2], convicted of one count of mail fraud by a jury on September 15, 2025 [2][4], and on March 10, 2026 was sentenced to 73 months in prison, 3 years of supervised release, and forfeiture of approximately $5.2 million, with restitution "to be determined later" [1]. At sentencing, prosecutors said the scheme ran from 2015 to 2022, involved "nearly three million" mailers, caused "losses of nearly $9 million to tens of thousands of victims," and that he "personally obtained at least approximately $5.2 million" [1].

## (c) DO NOT SAY

- **"More than three million" / "three million" as a hard number.** DOJ disagrees with itself: conviction release [4] and case page [2] say "over three million"; the later sentencing release [1] says "nearly three million." Say "nearly three million" or "about three million," and the title "Three Million Fake Invoices" should be softened (e.g. "Nearly Three Million…").
- **"He mailed three million invoices"** (sole actor). Every DOJ source says he "mailed, and caused others to mail" [1][2][4]; much of the volume went out under reseller companies [2][3].
- **"$2.99 million collected over six years" as the scheme's size.** That is the conviction-era figure for "approximately 2015 and 2021" [2][4]. Superseded at sentencing by "personally obtained at least approximately $5.2 million" and "losses of nearly $9 million" [1]. Pairing $2.99M "collected" with a $5.2M forfeiture (as scout.json does) reads as a contradiction. Use the sentencing figures [1].
- **"Six years."** Sentencing release: "seven-year scheme," "Between 2015 and 2022" [1]; conviction quote "more than seven years" [4]; indictment "March 2015 through … May 2022" [3].
- **"Sentenced in 2025."** Convicted Sept 15, 2025; sentenced **March 10, 2026** [1][2]. scout.json's "2025 (conviction/sentencing)" is wrong on the sentence.
- **"~$7 million / 38,000 payments" stated as fact.** Indictment allegation only [3] (and includes reseller receipts). If used: "the indictment alleged…".
- **"A website you already *owned*"** — sources say "small businesses that already had websites" [1][2] and "whose websites were hosted by other providers" [1]. Use "already had a website."
- **"Every mailer said INVOICE" / "the mailers had no disclaimer."** At least one DOJ exemplar says "NOT A BILL, INVOICE, OR STATEMENT OF ACCOUNT DUE" [2a], and Connecticut noted the "no obligation to pay" line [5]. Keep this — it's the story's twist, not a problem — but don't flatten it.
- **Victims got money back / refunds / restitution paid.** Restitution "in an amount to be determined later" [1]; nothing further in any source.
- **How he found targets (WHOIS scraping etc.).** Only a blogger's guess [7], "Most likely…". Not in any primary source.
- **Specific victim businesses, their losses, or their reactions.** No victim is named or quoted in any source. No invented victim scenes.
- **"The mailers threatened to take your site down."** Mailer text says failure to renew "may result in website outages and a loss of your online identity" [2a][5]. Quote that; don't escalate.
- **Trial length precision.** "one-week" [2][4] vs "six-day" [1] — just say "a jury trial" or "about a week."
- **thefederalnewswire.com** — scout's only secondary is a 404 [8]; cite nothing from it.

## (e) Verdict

**SHIP-WITH-CAVEATS** — The story rests on four consistent DOJ primaries (indictment, case page, conviction and sentencing releases) plus a contemporaneous Connecticut state warning, the villain is a convicted defendant, and the vivid detail is real and DOJ-published (two actual mailers, one of which literally says it is "NOT A BILL"). Caveats are all about scout.json's numbers: the mailer count is disputed within DOJ ("over" vs "nearly" three million → say "nearly"), the $2.99M/six-year figures are superseded by the sentencing figures ($5.2M obtained, ~$9M losses, 2015–2022), the sentence was in 2026 not 2025, and the scout's hook overstates by making him the sole mailer of "three million" to people who "owned" their sites.
