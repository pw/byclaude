# doordash-clone — verified brief (EVIDENCE seat, 2026-09-26)

Sources: `sources/1.txt`–`7.txt` (raw text, URL line 1, fetch note line 2), verbatim lines in `sources/EXCERPTS.md`, fetch problems in `sources/BLOCKED.md`.
Source weight: three independent same-week local news reports — CBS News Chicago [1] (2022-11-30), NBC Chicago [2] (2022-11-30), Block Club Chicago [3] (2022-12-02) — plus a WGN Radio interview page [4] and a 2024 NewsNation recap [5]. **There is no court, agency, or police record; nobody was identified or charged.** All facts below are reporting of what the owners and DoorDash said.

## (a) Verified summary

Smoque BBQ, at 3800 N. Pulaski Road in Chicago's Old Irving Park [3], was impersonated on DoorDash twice in the autumn of 2022. In October, someone "made a duplicate DoorDash Smoque account … and copied its menu," so customers were ordering from two listings, "though only one was connected to the real Smoque" [3]; co-owner Barry Sorkin said it took "several days" to fix [1], co-owner Mike McDermott said "about a week" [3]. Then, in the last days of November (Sorkin "went on the app on Tuesday" [1], in a story published Wednesday Nov. 30, 2022 — i.e. Tuesday Nov. 29), it happened again, worse: Smoque's "real page was taken down without Smoque knowing and someone created a fake account" [3] — "this new storefront was up and ours was gone" [3] — with "the same hours, same address and similar food items" [1]. The restaurant found out when DoorDash drivers started arriving "to pick up orders for orders we didn't have" [1] — "four or five orders" [3]. Sorkin said customers were "placing legitimate orders" as far as they could tell [2], and that he "presum[ed]" the payment was "getting funneled to this phantom site" [2]; but Block Club reported "It's not clear where the money from the fake Smoque page has gone" [3]. Smoque told customers on social media to stop ordering through DoorDash [2][3]. The fake page came down Wednesday Nov. 30 [3] — Block Club says after Smoque posted on social media [3], CBS says "shortly after CBS 2 reached out" [1]. DoorDash said it would "deactivate the fraudulent store and reinstate the correct restaurant" [1][2], and "will reimburse Smoque customers who placed orders from the fraudulent page and the company for orders it fulfilled" [3]; Smoque "decided to make up the missing orders and didn't lose that much money" [3]. The owners' standing complaint: to prove they were the real business they had to send DoorDash paperwork — "where were all those safeguards when whoever did this was making all the changes that resulted in the fraudulent site" [1]. DoorDash said it checks tax information and may request personal ID [1][3]. Same week, same city, same article [3]: Brown Sugar Bakery in Chatham — which isn't on DoorDash at all — had customers and drivers showing up with receipts from a fake DoorDash page (DoorDash says no payments were processed through that page [3]).

## (c) DO NOT SAY

Claims from scout.json (or natural embellishments) the sources do NOT support, or that are disputed:

1. **"DoorDash only removed the fake listing after CBS 2 contacted the company"** (scout summary) — DISPUTED. CBS [1] says only "taken down shortly after CBS 2 reached out" (sequence, not cause). Block Club [3] says "DoorDash only took down the fake page for Smoque on Wednesday after the owner posted about the problems on social media and asked customers not to use the platform." NBC [2] quotes Sorkin: "DoorDash has addressed this more quickly this time." McDermott [3]: "Within a day, they took care of it" Safe: "the fake page came down within about a day."
2. **"a perfect copy" / "perfect fake"** (scout title + hook) — overstated. Sorkin [2]: "their menu is the same. The photographer on the menu is different but the average person wouldn't know that" (sic — "photographer" as published; likely "photography"). CBS [1] says "similar food items," not identical. Safe: "the same name, hours and address, and the same menu — only the photos were different."
3. **"same full menu"** (scout summary) — CBS, the scout's own primary, says "similar food items." Attribute any "same menu" line to the owner [2] or to the October copy [3] ("copied its menu").
4. **"someone built a perfect copy of it inside the app, twice"** (scout why_it_hooks) — the two incidents were DIFFERENT: October was a duplicate beside the real page; November was a replacement — the real page was taken down and the fake stood in its place [3]. Don't describe both as the same clone.
5. **Anything about who got the money, a bank account, a payout, or the scammer profiting** — NO source says this. Block Club [3]: "It's not clear where the money from the fake Smoque page has gone." Sorkin's line [2] is explicitly a presumption ("presumable aren't going to get payment for because the payment is getting funneled to this phantom site"). NewsNation [5] ("The fraudster took orders and credit card payments online") is a 2024 secondary recap, not independent reporting. Safe: "customers paid — and the restaurant never saw the orders" + "where that money went, nobody has said."
6. **How the scammer took down the real page (hacked login, account takeover, stolen credentials, etc.)** — unknown. McDermott [3]: "it's not totally clear how they were able to have our storefront taken down."
7. **Any dollar figure for Smoque** — none exists. Do not invent "hundreds/thousands in orders." The only number is "four or five orders" [3]. "didn't lose that much money" [3].
8. **"DoorDash had no checks" / "no verification"** — DoorDash says it checks tax info, can request ID [1], and requires tax ID, business info, owner's name and ID "at times" [3]. The absence of safeguards is the OWNER's question; frame it as his question.
9. **"Customers lost their money"** — DoorDash said it will reimburse customers who ordered from the fraudulent page [3]. The CBS video blurb says "leaving some customers without their ribs and cash" [6] — true at the time; do not imply permanent loss.
10. **The scout's SF Chronicle link as corroboration of "the same period" / "the same vulnerability"** — that page is BLOCKED (not read). Search + SFist [7] show it is April 2021 (≈19 months earlier) and a different scheme: a ghost kitchen using a CLOSED restaurant's name (Blowfish Sushi) and a Tokyo brand (Wagyumafia). Not a clone of an operating business. If the film wants "not a one-off," use Brown Sugar Bakery [3] (same week, same city).
11. **October duration as a precise figure** — sources disagree ("several days" [1] vs "about a week" [3]). Say "days."
12. **"Second time in two months"** as a quoted figure — sources say "In October" [1], "second time in recent months" [3], "last month and then again just this week" [2]. "In October — and again in late November" is supported.
13. **The Brown Sugar Bakery $150 as a DoorDash-scam loss without the dispute** — it's the manager's figure for food handed out against fake receipts [3]; DoorDash said "no payments were actually processed" through that page [3]. If used, both sides go in.
14. **Any customer, driver, or DoorDash employee's feelings/dialogue** — not in sources. The "one customer on Facebook asking where his order was" [1] is the only customer detail; unnamed, no quote.
15. **Street-level location shots of Smoque** — per BRIEF rules, don't depict the real premises; the address is fine as a fact, not as an image.

## (e) Verdict

**SHIP-WITH-CAVEATS.** The core — a real Chicago BBQ joint impersonated on DoorDash twice in fall 2022, the second time with its real page removed and a fake standing in its place, discovered when drivers arrived for orders the kitchen never got, owners named and quoted, DoorDash on record — is supported by three independent contemporaneous reports, and the vivid detail (drivers at the counter for phantom orders) is in all three. Caveats that must hold in the VO: (1) no official record exists and the scammer is unidentified — the villain is the gap, not a person; (2) the money trail is UNKNOWN — the film cannot say who profited or how; (3) the "only after CBS called" framing is disputed and must be dropped; (4) not a "perfect" copy; (5) no dollar figure for Smoque, and DoorDash said it would reimburse customers and the restaurant — end on that, not on a loss.
