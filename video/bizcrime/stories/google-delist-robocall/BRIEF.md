# BRIEF — google-delist-robocall

Evidence seat, 2026-09-26. All claims below rest on `sources/EXCERPTS.md` (83 verbatim excerpts, each machine-checked against the fetched source text; a fabricated negative control correctly failed). Source numbers [n] match `sources/<n>.txt`.

**Case:** FTC v. Pointbreak Media, LLC, et al., No. 18-61017-CIV-ALTONAGA/McAliley (S.D. Fla.). The court record, not the press releases, is the authority here: the summary-judgment Report [10] and Order [8] are court *findings*; the complaints [5][9] are FTC *allegations*.

## (a) Verified summary

From November 2016 through May 2018, a Florida telemarketing operation led by Pointbreak Media, run day to day by Dustin Pillonato and Justin Ramsey, robocalled small businesses with a recorded "final notice" warning that "Google will label your business as permanently closed" unless the owner pressed one to reach a "Google specialist" [10]. Pointbreak had no affiliation with Google, and Google does not remove or close-label a business for not claiming its listing, a process that costs nothing [10][4]. In one seven-week stretch of summer 2017, Pointbreak alone made nearly 75 million robocalls, nearly 15 million of them to numbers on the Do Not Call Registry [10]. Owners who pressed one paid Pointbreak a one-time fee of $300 to $500 to "claim and verify" their listing [10] (the FTC put the range across all the companies at $300 to $700 [1]), and were then called back and sold a "Citation Program" for $499.99 to $849.99 plus $169.99 a month, which the court found produced no change in search results [10]. Pillonato admitted that clients "saw 0 benefits" and "got 0 results" [10]. When Bank of America Merchant Services cut off Pointbreak's card processing on October 20, 2017, the company spent October 23 to 27 writing itself $100 checks from at least 280 customers' bank accounts without consent, every one endorsed by Ramsey [10]. Both men had already been under court orders: Pillonato from a 2015 Florida injunction, Ramsey from an April 2017 federal injunction barring robocalls and calls to Do Not Call numbers, months before the 75 million calls [10]. They represented themselves, took the Fifth at deposition, lost on summary judgment on April 4, 2019 [8][10], and on April 25, 2019 were ordered to pay $3,367,666.30 and hand over a list of 53 watches, bracelets, gold coins and "all items ... contained in a safe" [3]. In August 2020 the FTC mailed refunds totaling more than $700,000 to 4,467 small business owners, averaging $158.32 [6].

## (b) Hook candidates

1. **"This is your final notice. If you do not act soon, Google will label your business as permanently closed."** — the robocall's own words, quoted by the court [10]. Recommended: it is literally true that the call said this, and the next line can be "It wasn't Google."
2. **"The call said your free Google listing was about to be shut down. It was right about one thing: the listing was free."** — rests on the script's own "Press one now to find out how to validate your free Google listing" ([9], FTC allegation, attribute it) + "not even Google charges businesses to claim, verify and manage their business listings" [10].
3. **"Nearly 75 million robocalls in seven weeks — and the man who wrote the script was already under a court order to stop."** — [10]: "nearly 75 million robocalls" July 3–Aug 25, 2017; "He drafted Pointbreak's robocall scripts" (Ramsey); April 2017 injunction barring him from robocalls/DNC calls.

Strong closing object: the court's list of 53 luxury items to be surrendered, ending with "All items ... contained in a safe" [3] — set against refunds averaging $158.32 [6].

## (e) Verdict

**SHIP-WITH-CAVEATS.** The story rests on three federal court documents (findings of undisputed fact, summary judgment, final order) plus four FTC releases — about as strong as primary sourcing gets. The villain is an adjudicated enterprise, not a private misfortune. The vivid details are better than the scout knew and are court-sourced: the "final notice" script, ~75M calls in seven weeks, the $100 checks written the week the card processor quit them, "saw 0 benefits", the prior injunctions, and the 53-item jewelry list ending in "contained in a safe." Caveats: the scout's specific numbers (74 million *people*, 14 million, $949.99, $700, 250) must be replaced with the court's figures, and "delete" must become the script's actual words.
