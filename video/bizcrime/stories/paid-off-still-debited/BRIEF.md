# BRIEF — paid-off-still-debited
Evidence seat, 2026-09-26. Sources: `sources/1.txt`–`11.txt`, verbatim lines in `sources/EXCERPTS.md`.
Primary sources fetched in full: FTC complaint + exhibits + stipulated order (S.D.N.Y. 1:20-cv-06023), three FTC press releases (2020/2021/2022), FTC case page, FTC refunds page, Chopra statement. No page was blocked.

## (a) Verified summary
On August 3, 2020 the FTC sued Yellowstone Capital LLC, Fundry LLC, founder and CEO Yitzhak D. Stern (a/k/a Isaac Stern) and president Jeffrey Reece in federal court in New York. The FTC alleged that Yellowstone sold merchant cash advances repaid by daily ACH debits from the business's bank account (its sample contract: $10,000 funded, $14,000 repaid, 25% of daily receipts), and that "since at least 2015" it kept debiting after businesses had fully repaid — typically "an additional 4-5 or more" daily payments, which the company attributed to a "lag" or "debit delay" ("there is a 4 day lag on ACH debits . . . it's simply the way our processor works"). The FTC said this hit "at least thousands" of customers for "hundreds to thousands of dollars" each and "at least millions of dollars" overall; one customer lost $3,480 in four extra payments, another $4,345 over "another two weeks," another $5,409; some were left overdrawn, and refunds, when given, often came only after complaints and "sometimes" took "weeks or months." The complaint also alleged Yellowstone held back hundreds-to-thousands in fees from the promised funding ("you guys are like highway robbery") and advertised "No collateral, no personal guarantee" while requiring both. In April 2021 the defendants, neither admitting nor denying the allegations, agreed to a court order with a $9,837,000 judgment and a ban on withdrawals without "express informed consent." In June 2022 the FTC sent 7,731 checks totaling more than $9.7 million — 51% of eligible businesses' money, averaging more than $1,200 each; the FTC later said the first two rounds (2022, 2024) "resulted in more than $9.1 million in refunds," and a third round of 3,759 checks (over $549,000) went out in 2026.

## (c) DO NOT SAY
1. **"for months" (scout's why_it_hooks: "kept taking money out of their account anyway — for months").** NOT SUPPORTED. The debits continued "for days" (2020 & 2021 releases), typically "4-5 or more" payments (complaint ¶30); the longest cited example is "for another two weeks" (¶32). What took "weeks or months" was the REFUND, not the debiting (¶34; 2022 release). Correct line: "for days after — sometimes two weeks."
2. **"hundreds or thousands of dollars a day" (scout summary).** NOT SUPPORTED as a per-day figure. Daily payments were "typically hundreds of dollars each day" (¶28); the overpayment *total per customer* ranged "hundreds to thousands" (¶29, ¶35).
3. **Title "$9.8 Million Taken From Bank Accounts That Were Already Paid Off."** FALSE as framed. $9,837,000 is the settlement judgment resolving ALL counts (overdebits + short-funding + collateral/guarantee ads). The complaint puts the overdebits at "at least millions of dollars" — no precise figure. Never equate $9.8M with the amount over-debited.
4. **"The FTC mailed $9.7 million back" as money businesses received.** Say "sent checks totaling more than $9.7 million" (2022 release). The refunds page (2026) says rounds one and two together "resulted in more than $9.1 million in refunds" — i.e., not every check was cashed. Sources differ; use "sent", not "returned/recovered". Also: refunds were 51% of eligible losses, not full repayment.
5. **"Charged" in a criminal sense / "convicted" / "guilty" / "fraud."** This is a CIVIL FTC lawsuit under the FTC Act (unfair/deceptive practices). No criminal charge, no admission: the order says defendants "neither admit nor deny." Say "the FTC alleged," "sued," "settled." (Chopra's statement and the 2021 release use "charging/charges" in the civil sense — don't import that word into VO without "the FTC.")
6. **"Fundry.com LLC" (scout).** WRONG. Complaint and order: "Fundry LLC." (The 2021 release says "Fundry Inc." — also a discrepancy; the court documents control.)
7. **"Principals" unnamed / name spellings.** Names are Yitzhak D. Stern a/k/a Isaac Stern (founder & CEO) and Jeffrey Reece (president) per the complaint. Ignore the case page's "Yithak" and the order's OCR "Stem."
8. **Anything about the NY Attorney General case ("billion-dollar case," $16.1M, debt cancellation, exiting the industry).** None of it is in this story's primary sources; the scout's secondary URL (ClaimDepot, source 4) is about that SEPARATE NYAG action, not the FTC case. Keep the two apart; if the script mentions the NYAG case at all it needs its own sourcing (not done here).
9. **COVID-era framing ("preyed on businesses during the pandemic").** Conduct alleged "since at least 2015"; the COVID context is Commissioner Chopra's personal statement (source 11), not an allegation. At most: "The FTC sued in August 2020."
10. **"Thousands of businesses were overcharged" as fact.** OK only as the FTC's allegation ("at least thousands," ¶29). The one hard count is 7,731 checks (2022).
11. **"Knowingly/deliberately stole."** The complaint alleges "Defendants have acknowledged that they take these overpayments from customers knowingly" — attribute it: "the FTC said the company knew." Do not state intent as fact.
12. **Case status "Pending"** on the FTC case page — page artifact; the case is resolved by the 2021 stipulated order.
13. **Exact order-entry date.** FTC filed the proposed order April 21, 2021; case page lists April 22, 2021; the court PDF footer reads "Filed 05/04/21." Say "in 2021" / "April 2021 settlement."
14. **Interior states / invented details** (the owner staring at the account, a named shop). No victim is named anywhere; all victim quotes are anonymous complaint excerpts.

## (e) Verdict
**SHIP-WITH-CAVEATS.** The story rests on the FTC's own complaint, court order and three agency releases, and the vivid details (the "4 day lag," $3,480 in four extra payments, "throw her the $100 to go away," the contract's no-responsibility-for-overdrafts clause) are verbatim in the primary court filing. Caveats: (1) the scout's hook "for months" is false — debits ran for days (up to two weeks cited); the REFUNDS took weeks or months; (2) everything is an allegation settled without admission; (3) $9.8M is the whole settlement, not the over-debited sum, and "$9.7M sent" ≠ "$9.7M received" (FTC later: >$9.1M from two rounds); (4) keep the NYAG case out.
