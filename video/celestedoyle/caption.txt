December 1872, the Atlantic between the Azores and Portugal. The brigantine Mary Celeste was found drifting with no one aboard. The "Marie Celeste" most people know comes from a young Arthur Conan Doyle, whose story ran unsigned in the Cornhill Magazine in 1884, three years before Sherlock Holmes. Told as a survivor's statement, it had the ship's boats still aboard, and even the American consul at Gibraltar asked whether it was true. In fact her only boat was gone, her cargo of alcohol was nearly untouched, and she carried food and water for six months. The story was invented. The empty ship was real. The ten people aboard were never found.

Still unsolved.
