In 1483, at the Tower of London, Edward V, aged 12, and his younger brother Richard, Duke of York, aged 9, were lodged inside the fortress while their uncle took the throne as Richard III. Through that summer the boys were seen less and less behind its walls, and after it there is no recorded sighting of either. In 1674, workmen at the Tower found the bones of two children under a staircase. Charles II had them laid in Westminster Abbey, where a marble urn still holds them. The bones were examined in 1933, but they have never been DNA tested, and whose they are has never been proven. The images are old paintings and 1890s photochrom prints, all public domain or CC0; the boys' faces are a painter's imagining.
