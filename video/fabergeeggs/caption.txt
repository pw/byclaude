A scrap metal dealer in the American Midwest paid about $14,000 for a small gold egg at a market, meaning to sell it for its gold. No buyer would pay his price. Years later, a web search for the watch inside, by Vacheron Constantin, led him to the London jeweller Wartski, which confirmed in 2012 what it was: the Third Imperial Egg, made by Fabergé in 1887 for Tsar Alexander III. It had been seized after the 1917 revolution, last recorded in Soviet hands in 1922, and sold at a New York auction in 1964 with no mention of Fabergé. Fifty Imperial eggs were made for the tsars. At least six have never been found.

Still unsolved.
