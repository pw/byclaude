Around 280 BC, in Alexandria, Egypt, the body of Alexander the Great was laid to rest in a coffin of gold. For centuries his tomb stood in the city. Julius Caesar came to see it, and Augustus laid a golden crown on the body. Around the year 400, the preacher John Chrysostom asked where the tomb of Alexander was; even his own people, he said, did not know. Records exist of more than 140 official searches, and all have failed. Theories place it beneath the modern city, in the desert, even in Venice.

Still unsolved.
