On 11 July 1897, three Swedes, S. A. Andrée, Nils Strindberg and Knut Frænkel, lifted off from Danes Island, Svalbard, in the hydrogen balloon Örnen, the Eagle, hoping to fly over the North Pole. Three days later, on 14 July, the balloon came down on the pack ice. After a week at the landing site, they set out on foot across the drifting ice. In early October the ice brought them to Kvitøya, White Island, and within days their diaries stop. In August 1930, Norwegian sealers found their last camp, and Strindberg's exposed film, recovered there that year, was developed in Stockholm 33 years after it was taken. Why all three men died so soon is still debated: illness from undercooked polar bear meat, cold and exhaustion, and other theories have all been proposed. The images are period photographs from 1897 and 1930 and one modern photograph of the island; the 1930 picture shows finds from the expedition on show in Stockholm. All are public domain or CC0.
