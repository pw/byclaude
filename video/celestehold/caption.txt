December 4, 1872, the Atlantic near the Azores. The men who boarded the empty brigantine Mary Celeste found a sounding rod on her deck, a tool for measuring water in the hold. Nearby, one of her two pumps had been partly taken apart. Two hatches stood open, and below was about three and a half feet of water, not enough to sink her. One theory is that a false reading convinced the captain she was sinking, and everyone left in her only boat. Her cargo of alcohol was nearly untouched, she had food and water for six months, and she was sailed on to Gibraltar. Not one of the ten people who had sailed on her was ever seen again.

Still unsolved.
