**Working it through**

The chili was cooked, cooled, and stored, and now it's being reheated to hot-hold it. FDA Food Code §3-403.11(A) covers exactly this case. Time/temperature control for safety (TCS) food that is cooked, cooled, and reheated for hot holding must be heated so that every part reaches at least **165°F (74°C) for 15 seconds**. The reheat also has to happen within 2 hours.

The other options come from different rules:
- **A) 135°F:** This is the hot-holding minimum (§3-501.16). It is also the reheat temperature in §3-403.11(C), but only for ready-to-eat food that was commercially processed and comes out of a sealed package. Chili cooked in your own kitchen is neither.
- **B) 145°F for 15 s:** This is the cooking temperature for intact cuts of meat, seafood, and eggs cooked for immediate service. It is not a reheat rule.
- **C) 155°F for 15 s:** This is the cooking temperature for ground or comminuted meat. Chili often contains ground beef, which makes this option tempting, but the question asks about reheating a food that was already cooked. The reheat rule is 165°F.
- **D) 165°F for 15 s:** This is correct.

**Is any other option defensible?** No. The 135°F exception needs food that was commercially processed and sealed, and this chili isn't. The microwave rule (§3-403.11(B)) is also 165°F, plus a 2-minute rest, and the question says the chili is reheated on the stove, so it doesn't apply. The oven rule for roasts (§3-403.11(E)) doesn't apply either. State food codes and recent Food Code editions (2013, 2017, 2022) all use 165°F/15 s for reheating food for hot holding. I didn't need an outside source for this: it's a core, stable rule.

**Are the numbers and units consistent?** Yes. All four options use °F with the same 15-second hold, and the question has no conflicting quantities.

ANSWER: D
