# fpm-handwash — sources

Film: this is the handwashing procedure. The question asks for the minimum total time. Answer: **C) 20 seconds**.
The question is original. It is not taken from any exam, vendor item bank or prep company.

## Rules and their authority

Both editions were read from FDA's own PDFs (fetched through the Wayback Machine copies of the fda.gov download URLs).
The section wording is the same in both editions. The only difference is "finger tips" in 2022 and "fingertips" in
2026. One more note: the slate cited 2-301.12 without paragraph letters, and in both editions the 20-second rule is
¶ (A) and the procedure is ¶ (B).

- FDA Food Code 2022 (January 18, 2023 version), Chapter 2 p. 2-18: https://www.fda.gov/media/164194/download
- FDA Food Code 2026, Chapter 2 p. 39: https://www.fda.gov/media/194741/download

1. **At least 20 seconds in total: § 2-301.12(A).**
   > "(A) Except as specified in ¶ (D) of this section, FOOD EMPLOYEES shall clean their hands and exposed portions
   > of their arms, including surrogate prosthetic devices for hands or arms for at least 20 seconds, using a
   > cleaning compound in a HANDWASHING SINK that is equipped as specified under § 5-202.12 and Subpart 6-301."
   (¶ (D) covers approved automatic handwashing facilities. That is why the question says "at a handwashing sink".)

2. **The procedure, in the order stated, including the 10 to 15 second rub: § 2-301.12(B).**
   > "(B) FOOD EMPLOYEES shall use the following cleaning procedure in the order stated to clean their hands and
   > exposed portions of their arms, including surrogate prosthetic devices for hands and arms:
   > (1) Rinse under clean, running warm water;
   > (2) Apply an amount of cleaning compound recommended by the cleaning compound manufacturer;
   > (3) Rub together vigorously for at least 10 to 15 seconds while: (a) Paying particular attention to removing
   > soil from underneath the fingernails during the cleaning procedure, and (b) Creating friction on the surfaces
   > of the hands and arms or surrogate prosthetic devices for hands and arms, finger tips, and areas between the
   > fingers;
   > (4) Thoroughly rinse under clean, running warm water; and
   > (5) Immediately follow the cleaning procedure with thorough drying using a method as specified under § 6-301.12."

   The on-screen steps paraphrase these five items. "Apply soap" stands for "apply … cleaning compound". "Dry right
   away" stands for "immediately follow … with thorough drying".

3. **Why the rub is timed: the FDA Food Code 2022, Annex 3 (public health reasons), § 2-301.12.**
   > "Research has shown a minimum 10-15 second scrub is necessary to remove transient pathogens from the hands and
   > when an antimicrobial soap is used, a minimum of 15 seconds is required."
   This is background only and not shown on screen. It does not change the 20-second total.

Checked but not used: the water temperature at a handwashing sink is at least 85°F. The 2022 and 2026 § 5-202.12(A)
both say: "A HANDWASHING SINK shall be equipped to provide water at a temperature of at least 29.4°C (85°F)". I left
it out of the film. A common "100°F" figure is not what the current code says.

## Worked answer

The rule gives the minimum total time directly: 20 seconds (§ 2-301.12(A)).
- The on-screen bars are drawn to scale against 20 seconds. The whole wash is a full bar: 20 of 20. The rubbing step
  is solid to 10 of 20 and lighter to 15 of 20, which shows the code's "at least 10 to 15 seconds". The bars show
  durations. They do not say when during the wash the rubbing happens.
- On-screen figures: "20 SEC", "10–15 SEC", "10–15 seconds" (step 3), "20" (trap line), and the options 10, 15, 20
  and 30 seconds. Step numbers 1–5 and countdown digits 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 are labels and a five-second timer, not data.
  "2-301.12" is the section cite.

Why each distractor is wrong:
- A) 10 seconds: this is the low end of the rubbing step, § 2-301.12(B)(3), not the total.
- B) 15 seconds: this is the high end of the rubbing step, not the total. It is the trap line on screen: "B) 15
  seconds is only the rubbing step. The whole wash is at least 20."
- D) 30 seconds: this is more than the minimum. The code's floor is "at least 20 seconds". The question asks for the
  minimum ("at least how long").

Solved cold: exactly one option matches the ¶ (A) minimum. Being pinned to the FDA Food Code and a handwashing sink
removes the automatic-facility exception. Local codes adopt different Food Code editions, so the end card says "your
local code may differ". The 2022 and 2026 texts agree.
