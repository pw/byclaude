The answer is **C: 20 seconds**. I answered from the FDA Food Code text I know; I didn't look it up online for this one.

**Working it out**

FDA Food Code §2-301.12 (Cleaning Procedure) says:

- **(B):** a food employee must clean their hands and exposed parts of their arms, including surrogate prosthetic devices, "for at least 20 seconds."
- **(C):** this is the step-by-step procedure. Rinse under clean running warm water, apply soap, then "rub together vigorously for **at least 10 to 15 seconds**." After that, rinse thoroughly and dry.

So the whole procedure takes at least 20 seconds, and the scrubbing step inside it takes 10–15 seconds. ServSafe and the other ANSI-accredited exams teach it the same way: "20 seconds total, 10–15 seconds scrubbing."

**Could another option be right?**

- **A (10 s) and B (15 s):** These are the ends of the 10–15 second scrubbing range, so a careless reader might pick one. But the question asks for the time "in total" for the whole cleaning. That wording clearly points to the 20-second figure in paragraph (B), not the scrub step.
- **D (30 s):** No Food Code edition uses 30 seconds. The 2009, 2013, 2017 and 2022 editions all say 20 seconds total with 10–15 seconds of scrubbing. Some state codes lag the current FDA edition, but none uses 30 seconds for this procedure. The only similar number is the CDC's consumer advice to scrub for "at least 20 seconds." That matches C and doesn't support A, B or D.

**Numbers and units:** Everything is in seconds and nothing conflicts. The question says "hands and exposed arms," which matches the Code's own wording.

ANSWER: C
AMBIGUOUS: no
