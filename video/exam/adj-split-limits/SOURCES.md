# adj-split-limits — sources

Film: auto liability split limits, example limits 25/50/25 (labelled `example` on the question card; the end card
says `Rules vary by state.`). Original question — not taken from any exam, vendor item bank or prep company.

## Rules and their authority

1. **How split limits work: each-person cap, each-accident cap for all bodily injury "subject to" the each-person cap,
   and a separate property-damage cap per accident.** Texas Transportation Code §601.072(a-1) (the state statute
   setting Texas minimum limits; the structure is the same for any split limit, only the amounts differ).
   URL: https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TN/htm/TN.601.htm (legislature's static copy of Chapter 601)
   > "(1) $30,000 for bodily injury to or death of one person in one collision; (2) $60,000 for bodily injury to or
   > death of two or more persons in one collision, subject to the amount provided by Subdivision (1) for bodily
   > injury to or death of one of the persons; and (3) $25,000 for damage to or destruction of property of others in
   > one collision."

2. **The notation (per person / per accident / property), read in thousands.** Texas Department of Insurance consumer
   guide. URL: https://www.tdi.texas.gov/pubs/consumer/cb020.html
   > "Texas law requires you to have at least $30,000 of coverage for injuries per person, up to a total of $60,000
   > per accident, and $25,000 of coverage for property damage. This is called 30/60/25 coverage."
   Same page, on what happens above the limits: "If you don't have enough liability coverage to pay for the damages
   and injuries you cause, you might have to pay the rest out of your own pocket."

3. **Same notation from a second regulator, with a 25/50 bodily-injury pair.** Nevada Division of Insurance.
   URL: https://doi.nv.gov/Consumers/Automobile-Insurance/
   > "$25,000 in bodily injury per person, $50,000 in bodily injury per accident, and $20,000 in property damage
   > ("25/50/20")."
   > "Liability insurance coverage protects you only if you are liable for an accident and pays for the injuries to
   > others or damages to their property."
   (Why the question says the insured is "fully liable": liability coverage pays only what the insured is liable for.)

State minimums differ (Texas 30/60/25, Nevada 25/50/20 …), so 25/50/25 is presented as example limits written into
the question, not as any state's minimum.

## Worked example (illustrative figures)

Limits 25/50/25 = $25,000 each person (bodily injury) · $50,000 each accident (all bodily injury) · $25,000 property
damage each accident.

Injuries (bodily injury), each person first:
- person 1: 30,000 > 25,000 each-person cap → 25,000
- person 2: 20,000 ≤ 25,000 → 20,000
- person 3: 10,000 ≤ 25,000 → 10,000
- sum: 25,000 + 20,000 + 10,000 = 55,000 > 50,000 each-accident cap → 50,000

Property (the other cars): 28,000 > 25,000 property cap → 25,000

Total: 50,000 + 25,000 = 75,000  → answer **B) $75,000**

The distractors, each wrong for a stated reason:
- A) $50,000 — pays the bodily-injury maximum only; ignores the separate property-damage limit (50,000 + 0).
- C) $78,000 — pays the property damage in full; its cap is 25,000 (50,000 + 28,000 = 78,000).
- D) $80,000 — applies the each-person cap but forgets the each-accident cap: 25,000 + 20,000 + 10,000 = 55,000,
  + 25,000 = 80,000. This is the trap line on screen: "D) $80,000 forgets the each-accident cap: injuries together
  max out at $50,000."
(Skipping the each-person cap but keeping the each-accident cap gives 30,000 + 20,000 + 10,000 = 60,000 → 50,000,
+ 25,000 = 75,000 — the same right total, so no option rewards that error.)

Other numbers on screen: option letters' amounts 50,000 / 75,000 / 78,000 / 80,000 (above); "person 1", "person 2",
"person 3" (labels); countdown ring digits 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 (a five-second pause timer, not data).

## Cold solve (builder, after writing)
Exactly one option survives: B. The limits are written into the question, so no state difference changes the
answer. "Fully liable" removes a comparative-fault reading; "liability coverage … for these damages" rules out
med-pay/collision and supplementary payments (defense costs, which standard forms pay in addition to the limits).
