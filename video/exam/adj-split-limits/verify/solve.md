**The limits.** 25/50/25 is a standard split limit:
- **$25,000** bodily injury (BI) per person
- **$50,000** BI per accident, covering all injured people combined
- **$25,000** property damage (PD) per accident

**Step 1: Cap each injured person at $25,000.**
- Person 1 has $30,000 in damages, so the policy pays $25,000. The $5,000 left over is the insured's own responsibility.
- Person 2 has $20,000, so the policy pays $20,000.
- Person 3 has $10,000, so the policy pays $10,000.
- Those add up to $55,000.

**Step 2: Apply the $50,000 per-accident BI cap.**
- $55,000 is over the cap, so total BI paid is **$50,000**.

**Step 3: Apply the $25,000 PD cap.**
- The cars have $28,000 in damage, so the policy pays **$25,000**. The $3,000 left over is uncovered.

**Total:** $50,000 + $25,000 = **$75,000**

**Why the other options are wrong:**
- **A ($50,000)** counts only the bodily injury.
- **C ($78,000)** pays the full $28,000 for property damage and ignores the $25,000 PD limit.
- **D ($80,000)** looks like $55,000 of BI plus $25,000 of PD, or else each person capped at $25,000 with the $50,000 aggregate ignored.

**Is any other option defensible?** No. Under a split-limit reading, both per-accident caps apply no matter how the $50,000 BI is shared among the three people. How the insurer divides that $50,000 is a real claims-handling question, but it doesn't change the total.

The only other reading would treat "25/50/25" as a combined single limit, and no standard reading does that. Defense costs are paid in addition to the limits under supplementary payments, but the question asks only about damages. Some states have different minimum limits, but that doesn't matter here because the question states the limits.

**Are the numbers consistent?** Yes. They are designed so that each cap binds once: one person over the $25,000 per-person cap ($30,000), the injury total over the $50,000 per-accident cap ($55,000), and the property damage over the $25,000 PD cap ($28,000). All figures are in dollars, and nothing contradicts anything else.
