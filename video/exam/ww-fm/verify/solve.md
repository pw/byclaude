**The work**

F/M is the pounds of food (BOD) entering per day divided by the pounds of microorganisms under aeration. The microorganisms are measured as MLVSS. The ABC Wastewater formula sheet and the Sacramento State (Kenneth Kerri) operator manuals both define it this way:

- **Food (BOD, lbs/day)** = 1.5 MGD × 8.34 lb/gal × 160 mg/L = **2,001.6 lbs/day**
- **Microorganisms (MLVSS, lbs)** = 0.5 MG × 8.34 lb/gal × 2,400 mg/L = **10,008 lbs**
- **F/M** = 2,001.6 ÷ 10,008 = **0.20** (answer C)

The 8.34 factor appears in both the top and bottom of the ratio, so it cancels: (1.5 × 160) ÷ (0.5 × 2,400) = 240 ÷ 1,200 = 0.20.

**The other options**

- **B) 0.16** comes from using MLSS instead of MLVSS: 2,001.6 ÷ (0.5 × 8.34 × 3,000 = 12,510) = 0.16. This is the planted trap. Some older operator materials use MLSS as a stand-in for the organisms, but only when MLVSS isn't known. Here MLVSS is given outright, and the standard formula sheets specify MLVSS. B would only be defensible under a state formula sheet that defines F/M on MLSS. I don't know of a current one that does, and I didn't check state sheets with a web search.
- **D) 5.00** is the ratio flipped (M/F = 10,008 ÷ 2,001.6 = 5.0).
- **A) 0.07** doesn't follow from any reasonable combination of the numbers. It's a plain distractor.

**Consistency check**

The units are consistent (MG, MGD and mg/L throughout). The numbers are realistic: MLVSS is 80% of MLSS, which is typical. An F/M of 0.2 falls in the normal range for conventional activated sludge, and the detention time works out to 8 hours. Nothing in the question conflicts.

ANSWER: C
