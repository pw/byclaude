# adj-equal-shares — sources

Film: "other insurance" — two primary liability policies sharing one loss by contribution by equal shares.
Original question — not taken from any exam, vendor item bank or prep company. Figures are labelled `example`.

## Rule and its authority

1. **Contribution by equal shares, and the fallback (contribution by limits).** ISO Commercial General Liability
   Coverage Form CG 00 01 04 13, SECTION IV – COMMERCIAL GENERAL LIABILITY CONDITIONS, 4. Other Insurance,
   c. Method Of Sharing. Standard policy form, copy hosted by the County of Sonoma (sample ISO CGL form):
   URL: https://sonomacounty.gov/Main%20County%20Site/General/Sonoma/Sample%20Dept/Sample%20Dept/Divisions%20and%20Sections/Liability/Services/Help%20Request/Subpages/Help%20Request/_Documents/SampleISO-CGL.pdf
   > "If all of the other insurance permits contribution by equal shares, we will follow this method also. Under
   > this approach each insurer contributes equal amounts until it has paid its applicable limit of insurance or
   > none of the loss remains, whichever comes first."
   > "If any of the other insurance does not permit contribution by equal shares, we will contribute by limits.
   > Under this method, each insurer's share is based on the ratio of its applicable limit of insurance to the
   > total applicable limits of insurance of all insurers."

2. **Why the question says both policies are primary.** Same form, 4. Other Insurance, a. Primary Insurance:
   > "This insurance is primary except when Paragraph b. below applies. If this insurance is primary, our
   > obligations are not affected unless any of the other insurance is also primary. Then, we will share with all
   > that other insurance by the method described in Paragraph c. below."
   (If one policy were excess, it would not share at all until the primary is used up — so "primary" is pinned.)

On-screen rule sentence is a plain-English paraphrase of quote 1; on-screen cite: `CG 00 01 · OTHER INSURANCE`
(form number CG 00 01, edition 04 13).

## Worked example (illustrative figures)

Claim (covered loss) 120,000. Policy X limit 50,000. Policy Y limit 200,000. Both primary, both permit equal shares.

Equal shares — each pays the same amount until one reaches its limit:
- each pays 50,000 → X has now paid 50,000 = its limit (X is full); Y has paid 50,000 (limit 200,000, not reached)
- paid so far: 50,000 + 50,000 = 100,000; still owed: 120,000 - 100,000 = 20,000
- the rest: X pays 0 (at its limit); Y pays 20,000 (Y total 70,000 ≤ 200,000 limit)
- totals: X 50,000 + 0 = 50,000 · Y 50,000 + 20,000 = 70,000 · check 50,000 + 70,000 = 120,000

Answer **B) X $50,000 · Y $70,000**

Distractors, each wrong for a stated reason:
- A) X $60,000 · Y $60,000 — splits 120,000 / 2 = 60,000 each, ignoring X's 50,000 limit (X cannot pay 60,000).
- C) X $24,000 · Y $96,000 — contribution by LIMITS: total limits 50,000 + 200,000 = 250,000; X 50,000 / 250,000 =
  20% → 0.20 × 120,000 = 24,000; Y 200,000 / 250,000 = 80% → 0.80 × 120,000 = 96,000. That method applies only if a
  policy does NOT permit equal shares (quote 1). This is the trap line on screen: "C) splits by limits (X 20%, Y 80%), the fallback
  only if a policy won't share equally."
- D) X $50,000 · Y $50,000 — stops sharing when X runs out, leaving 20,000 unpaid; the form says each insurer keeps
  contributing until it reaches its limit "or none of the loss remains".

Other numbers on screen: countdown ring digits 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 (a five-second pause timer, not data); "0" = X's share
of the rest (above).

## Cold solve (builder, after writing)
Exactly one option survives: B. Both policies are stated primary and both permit equal shares, so the form's own
fallback (by limits) cannot apply; no state variation changes a policy's contract method. A sums to 120,000 but
breaks X's limit; C uses the wrong method; D leaves 20,000 of a covered loss unpaid under limits that could pay it.
No deductibles or defense costs are stated; none would move a different option to correct.
