**Rule.** This is the standard ISO CGL "Method of Sharing" condition (Section IV, Condition 4.c). When every policy allows contribution by equal shares, each insurer pays the same amount until the claim is paid in full or an insurer reaches its limit. Once one insurer's limit is used up, the others keep paying in equal shares, and a single remaining insurer pays the balance up to its own limit.

**Working the split:**
1. Both insurers pay equally until the smaller limit is used up. Policy X's limit is $50,000, so X pays $50,000 and Y pays $50,000. That covers $100,000 of the claim.
2. X is now exhausted. $120,000 − $100,000 = $20,000 is still owed, and Policy Y pays it alone.
3. **Totals: X pays $50,000 and Y pays $70,000.** Both are within their limits, and together they pay $120,000.

**Why the other options are wrong:**
- **A** ($60,000 / $60,000) is a pure 50/50 split with no attention to limits. It would have X pay $60,000, which is more than its $50,000 limit.
- **C** ($24,000 / $96,000) is pro rata by limits: X's limit is 50 of the 250 total, or 20%, and 20% of $120,000 is $24,000. The CGL uses that method only when some policy does not allow equal shares. The question says both policies do, so C is the classic wrong answer planted to catch people who mix up the two methods.
- **D** ($50,000 / $50,000) stops at the first step and leaves $20,000 unpaid, even though Y still has plenty of limit left.

**Could another answer be defended?** No. The question clearly says equal shares, which rules out C, and no reasonable reading supports A or D. The numbers are consistent: the claim is larger than the smaller limit and smaller than the combined $250,000, so the "equal until one is used up" step actually matters. The units are consistent too.

ANSWER: B
