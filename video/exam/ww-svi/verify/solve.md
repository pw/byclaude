**Solution**

The sludge volume index (SVI) is the volume in mL taken up by 1 gram of sludge after 30 minutes of settling. This is the standard textbook formula, so I didn't look it up:

SVI (mL/g) = [settled sludge volume (mL/L) × 1,000 mg/g] ÷ MLSS (mg/L)

SVI = (250 × 1,000) ÷ 2,000 = 250,000 ÷ 2,000 = **125 mL/g**

As a sanity check, 2,000 mg/L of MLSS is 2 g of solids in each litre. Those 2 g take up 250 mL, so 1 g takes up 125 mL. An SVI of about 100–150 is a normal, well-settling sludge, so the result is plausible.

**Why the other options are wrong**
- **A) 0.125:** 250 ÷ 2,000. It leaves out the 1,000 mg/g conversion.
- **B) 8:** 2,000 ÷ 250. The ratio is upside down and the units are wrong.
- **D) 8,000:** (2,000 × 1,000) ÷ 250. The ratio is upside down again.

**Is any other option defensible?** No. SVI is defined the same way in every state and every edition of the Sacramento State (OWP) manuals and the ABC/WEF references. There is a related index, the sludge density index (SDI = 100/SVI, which here would be 0.8 g/100 mL), but 0.8 isn't one of the choices, so it can't cause confusion.

**Are the numbers and units consistent?** Yes. Giving the settled volume in mL/L is the standard way to report a 1-litre settleometer result, MLSS in mg/L is standard, and the answers are in the correct unit, mL/g.
