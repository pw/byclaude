**Source:** FDA Food Code 2022 (January 18, 2023 version), §3-501.19, from https://www.fda.gov/media/164194/download. I checked every claim against the section text.

**Keyed answer C) 3:00 PM is correct.**
- §3-501.19(B)(1): the food "shall have an initial temperature of … 57°C (135°F) or greater when removed from hot holding temperature control." The pizza held at 135°F meets that.
- §3-501.19(B)(4): the food "shall be cooked and served, served at any temperature if READY-TO-EAT, or discarded, within 4 hours from the point in time when the FOOD is removed from temperature control."
- 11:00 AM + 4 hours = 3:00 PM. The worked clock (11:00 AM + 4 hours = 3:00 PM) matches.

**"Mark the pan; serve or toss by then" is correct.**
- §3-501.19(B)(3): the food "shall be marked or otherwise identified to indicate the time that is 4 hours past the point in time when the FOOD is removed from temperature control."
- §3-501.19(B)(5): food "in unmarked containers or PACKAGES, or marked to exceed a 4-hour limit shall be discarded."

**The trap line for D) 5:00 PM is correct.**
- §3-501.19(C)(1): the food "shall have an initial temperature of 5ºC (41ºF) or less when removed from temperature control and the FOOD temperature may not exceed 21ºC (70ºF) within a maximum time period of 6 hours."
- §3-501.19(C)(4)(a): the food shall be "Discarded if the temperature of the food exceeds 21°C (70°F)."
- So the 6-hour option really is cold-start only, and hot-held pizza can't use it. The on-screen wording ("cold food only (41°F or below at the start), tossed if it goes above 70°F") is a fair summary.
- The Code does allow one exception to the 41°F start, but only under the 4-hour option: cut fruit and vegetables, or hermetically sealed ready-to-eat food, may start at 70°F or below (§3-501.19(B)(2)). It doesn't apply to pizza or to the 6-hour option, so the line isn't wrong.

**Distractors:** A) 1:00 PM (2 hours) and B) 2:00 PM (3 hours) are not the correct answer under the Code. You could argue that serving earlier is also compliant, but the question asks for the deadline ("by what time must it be…"), so they are clearly wrong.

**Rules that vary by state:** Some states' codes differ from the FDA model, for example by requiring approval before using time as a control or by not adopting the 6-hour option. The on-screen line "Based on the FDA Food Code; your local code may differ" covers this, and the question is framed the way the national food manager exams test it.
