**How I worked it out**

1. **The rule.** FDA Food Code 3-501.19(B) covers time as a public health control with a 4-hour limit. The food has to be at 135°F or above when it comes out of temperature control, which this pizza is. It must then be served or thrown out within 4 hours of that point. Any food left unserved at 4 hours gets discarded.
2. **The clock.** The pizza comes off hot holding at 11:00 AM. Adding 4 hours gives **3:00 PM**.

**Why the other options are wrong**

- **A) 1:00 PM** is only 2 hours. That's the first check in the two-stage cooling rule (135°F down to 70°F within 2 hours), not a time-control limit.
- **B) 2:00 PM** is 3 hours, which doesn't match any time-control limit.
- **D) 5:00 PM** is 6 hours. That limit is in 3-501.19(C), and it only applies to *cold* food that starts at 41°F or below and never goes above 70°F. Hot-held pizza can't use it. This is the trap option.

**Is another answer defensible?**

No, not under a reasonable reading.
- The only 6-hour option in the Food Code is for cold food, so it can't make D correct for hot food.
- Some states still use older codes that set hot holding at 140°F. That doesn't change the answer, because the pizza is already hot-held and the 4-hour limit is the same.
- A state that doesn't allow time as a public health control at all (or requires written procedures first) would make the question moot. It wouldn't make another option correct.

**Are the numbers and units consistent?**

Yes. 135°F is the current Food Code hot-holding minimum (it has been since the 2013 edition), and the times are all simple clock times.
