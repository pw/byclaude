**Recomputed**
- Weir length = 3.14 × 50 = 157 ft. That's the circumference, because the question says the weir runs around the full outer edge.
- Flow = 1.8 × 1,000,000 = 1,800,000 gpd.
- Rate = 1,800,000 ÷ 157 = 11,464.97, which rounds to 11,465 gpd/ft. This matches C.

**Distractors**
- A) 36,000 = 1,800,000 ÷ 50. This divides by the diameter instead of the circumference. It's wrong, and it's a realistic mistake.
- B) 22,930 = 1,800,000 ÷ (3.14 × 25). This uses the radius where circumference needs the diameter. It's wrong (the exact value is 22,929.9) and realistic.
- D) 917 = 1,800,000 ÷ (0.785 × 50²) = 1,800,000 ÷ 1,962.5. This divides by the tank's area, which gives surface overflow rate in gpd/ft². None of the three is also a correct answer.

**THE TRAP line**
"D) 917 divides by the tank's area. That is surface overflow rate, not weir overflow rate." This is correct. The formula sheet defines surface overflow rate as flow divided by area, in gpd/ft², and weir overflow rate as flow divided by weir length, in gpd/ft.

**Against the certifying body's sheet**
I checked the 2023 Wastewater Formula/Conversion Table from Water Professionals International (the successor to ABC), hosted by NEIWPCC:
- "Weir Overflow Rate, gpd/ft = Flow, gpd / Weir Length, ft"
- "Surface Loading Rate or Surface Overflow Rate, gpd/ft2 = Flow, gpd / Area, ft2"
- "Circumference of Circle = (3.14)(Diameter)"
- "π or pi = 3.14"

The video's method, its value for pi and its units all match the sheet. It's also right to state 3.14 in the question text.
