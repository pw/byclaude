# adj-depreciation-holdback — sources

Film: replacement-cost homeowners claim on a roof, insured has not yet replaced it. First payment = actual cash value
(ACV) minus the deductible; the recoverable depreciation is held back until the roof is replaced. All figures are an
illustrative example (labelled `example` on the question card; the end card says `Rules vary by state.`). Original
question — not taken from any exam, vendor item bank or prep company.

## Rules and their authority

1. **Replacement-cost loss settlement pays no more than ACV until the repair/replacement is actually done.**
   ISO Homeowners 3 – Special Form, HO 00 03 10 00, Section I Conditions, C. Loss Settlement, 2.d (the standard
   policy form; specimen hosted by the Insurance Information Institute).
   URL: https://www.iii.org/sites/default/files/docs/pdf/HO3_sample.pdf
   > "2. Buildings covered under Coverage A or B at replacement cost without deduction for depreciation, subject to
   > the following:"
   > "d. We will pay no more than the actual cash value of the damage until actual repair or replacement is
   > complete. Once actual repair or replacement is complete, we will settle the loss as noted in 2.a. and b. above."
   Small-loss exception, same clause — does NOT apply here ($12,000 is not less than $2,500):
   > "However, if the cost to repair or replace the damage is both: (1) Less than 5% of the amount of insurance in
   > this policy on the building; and (2) Less than $2,500; we will settle the loss as noted in 2.a. and b. above
   > whether or not actual repair or replacement is complete."
   Insurance-to-value condition (why the question says "insured to full value" — otherwise 2.b's proportional
   formula could apply):
   > "a. If, at the time of loss, the amount of insurance in this policy on the damaged building is 80% or more of
   > the full replacement cost of the building immediately before the loss, we will pay the cost to repair or
   > replace, after application of any deductible and without deduction for depreciation ..."
   Edition note: quoted from the 10 00 edition (the specimen I could fetch in full). I could not fetch a verbatim copy
   of the newer HO 00 03 05 11; the ACV-until-replaced structure is the long-standing ISO wording, and the question
   pins "standard replacement-cost terms" rather than an edition.

2. **The deductible comes off the loss payable.** Same form, Section I, Deductible:
   > "Subject to the policy limits that apply, we will pay only that part of the total of all loss payable under
   > Section I that exceeds the deductible amount shown in the Declarations."

3. **A regulator stating the same payment order: ACV less the deductible first, the rest as the work is done.**
   Florida Statutes §627.7011(3), Florida Legislature.
   URL: http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0600-0699/0627/Sections/0627.7011.html
   > "For a dwelling, the insurer must initially pay at least the actual cash value of the insured loss, less any
   > applicable deductible. The insurer shall pay any remaining amounts necessary to perform such repairs as work is
   > performed and expenses are incurred."
   ("at least" — a state may let or make an insurer pay more up front; that is why the question pins standard policy
   terms and the end card says rules vary by state.)

4. **ACV = replacement cost minus depreciation; depreciation as wear per year of age; a roof example with the
   deductible off the ACV payment and the difference paid on a receipt.** Tennessee Department of Commerce &
   Insurance consumer page.
   URL: https://www.tn.gov/commerce/insurance/consumer-edu/homeowners/cash-replacement.html
   > "Actual Cash Value policies pay consumers for replacement costs minus any applicable depreciation."
   > "Depreciation can be thought of as 'wear and tear' or age."
   > "the insurance company may determine that $500 of wear and tear ('depreciation') occurred each year since the
   > roof was installed. At the time of the claim, the company would pay $6,500. Depreciation would have reduced the
   > payment by $2,500 (5 years multiplied by $500 wear and tear per year), and the $1,000 deductible would have
   > reduced the payment by another $1,000."
   > "(Note: Insurance companies may initially pay the actual cash value for the roof, $6,500, and then reimburse
   > the difference following submission of a receipt.)"
   (Their $10,000 roof at $500 a year is itself straight-line over a 20-year life. Depreciation methods vary by
   insurer and state, so the question states "straight-line depreciation" outright.)

## Worked example (illustrative figures)

Given: replacement cost 12,000 · useful life 20 years · age 8 years · deductible 1,000 · roof not yet replaced ·
home insured to full value (80% condition met) · loss well over 2,500 (small-loss exception does not apply).

Depreciation (straight-line): 12,000 × 8 ÷ 20 = 96,000 ÷ 20 = 4,800   (8 of 20 years used = 40%)

Actual cash value: 12,000 – 4,800 = 7,200
Deductible:        7,200 – 1,000 = 6,200   → first payment **B) $6,200**
Held back (recoverable depreciation), paid after the roof is replaced: + 4,800
Total over the claim: 6,200 + 4,800 = 11,000 (= 12,000 – 1,000, replacement cost less the deductible)

The distractors, each wrong for a stated reason:
- A) $3,800 — treats the depreciation as what gets paid: 4,800 – 1,000 = 3,800. The depreciation is the part held
  back, not the part paid.
- C) $7,200 — the ACV with no deductible taken (12,000 – 4,800 = 7,200). The deductible applies to the loss payable.
- D) $11,000 — replacement cost less the deductible (12,000 – 1,000 = 11,000): pays the 4,800 of depreciation before
  the roof is replaced. This is the trap line on screen: "D) $11,000 pays out the held-back $4,800 before the roof
  is replaced." 11,000 is what the insured can receive in total, after replacement.

Other numbers on screen: "8" and "20" (years, from the question); countdown ring digits 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 (a five-second
pause timer, not data).

## Cold solve (builder, after writing)
Exactly one option survives: B. Straight-line depreciation and the figures are written into the question; "insured
to full value" removes the 80% proportional formula; $12,000 is above the $2,500 small-loss exception; "standard
replacement-cost terms" pins ACV-until-replaced (a state rule forcing more up front is outside the stated terms).
C would need the deductible to be deferred to the holdback — the form deducts it from the loss payable and the
Florida statute and Tennessee example both take it off the first (ACV) payment.
