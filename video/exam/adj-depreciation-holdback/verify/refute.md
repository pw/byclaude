**Distractors (each is a real mistake, and none is also correct):**
- A) $3,800 = 4,800 − 1,000. This takes the deductible off the depreciation instead of off the ACV.
- C) $7,200 is the ACV with the deductible forgotten.

**Policy basis:**
- **Holdback until replacement.** The ISO HO 00 03 10 00 form ([NV DOI copy](https://docs.nv.gov/doi/documents/home_policies/CSAAForms/AAA_HO_00_03_10_00.pdf)), Loss Settlement 2.d: *"We will pay no more than the actual cash value of the damage until actual repair or replacement is complete."*
- **The small-loss exception doesn't apply.** The same clause pays full replacement cost straight away only if the damage is *"(1) Less than 5% of the amount of insurance in this policy on the building; and (2) Less than $2,500."* $12,000 fails the $2,500 test, so the holdback stands. The question doesn't need to state the policy limit.
- **Deductible is taken from the amount payable.** Same form: *"we will pay only that part of the total of all loss payable under Section I that exceeds the deductible amount."* So ACV − deductible is right for the first payment.
- **"Insured to full value"** correctly rules out the 80% insurance-to-value penalty.

**Nits (not errors):**
1. **"FIRST PAYMENT … depreciation –"** has no 4,800 next to it, and **"deductible 1,000"** has no minus sign. If the screen really looks like that, the subtraction block shows "12,000, –, 7,200 … 1,000 … 6,200", and the viewer has to carry 4,800 over from the previous panel. Suggest: `replacement cost 12,000 / – depreciation 4,800 / = actual cash value 7,200 / – deductible 1,000 / = paid now 6,200`.
2. **"AFTER REPLACEMENT recoverable depreciation +"** shows no amount. Suggest `+ 4,800 once the roof is replaced (up to the cost actually incurred)`.
3. **"WHAT'S THE FIRST CHECK?"** puns on "check" as a payment, but it could be read as "what do you verify first". The question text clears this up, so it's style only.

**State variation (covered by the footer):** Several states don't allow depreciating the labor part of the cost when working out ACV. By statute: Arkansas. By court ruling: Illinois. By bulletin: Michigan and Wyoming. Others by case law ([HKR survey](https://www.hkr.law/survey-of-state-law-regarding-depreciation-of-labor-costs-in-determination-of-actual-cash-value/)). In those states the ACV would be higher than 7,200. The exam's straight-line method on the full replacement cost is the standard textbook convention, the card is labelled "example", and it carries "Rules vary by state." So this doesn't need a fix.
