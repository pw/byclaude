# fpm-cooling — sources

Film: two-stage cooling of cooked TCS food, as a timeline question (example start time 2:00 PM, labelled `example`
on the question card; the end card says `Based on the FDA Food Code; your local code may differ.`). Original
question — not taken from any exam, vendor item bank or prep company.

## Rules and their authority

1. **The two-stage cooling limits for cooked TCS food.** FDA Food Code 2022, § 3-501.14(A) (Chapter 3 - 26).
   URL: https://www.fda.gov/media/164194/download (read via https://web.archive.org/web/2024id_/https://www.fda.gov/media/164194/download —
   fda.gov serves an abuse-detection page to our datacenter IP; the archived file is FDA's own PDF, "January 18, 2023 Version")
   > "3-501.14 Cooling.
   > (A) Cooked time/temperature control for safety food shall be cooled:
   > (1) Within 2 hours from 57ºC (135ºF) to 21ºC (70°F); P and
   > (2) Within a total of 6 hours from 57ºC (135ºF) to 5ºC (41°F) or less. P"

2. **Unchanged in the newest edition.** FDA Food Code 2026 (released 2026-09-17), § 3-501.14(A), page 70–71.
   URL: https://www.fda.gov/media/194741/download (read via https://web.archive.org/web/2026id_/https://www.fda.gov/media/194741/download)
   > "(A) Cooked *TIME/TEMPERATURE CONTROL FOR SAFETY FOOD shall be cooled:
   > (1) Within two hours from 57ºC (135ºF) to 21ºC (70ºF); P and
   > (2) Within a total of six hours from 57ºC (135ºF) to 5ºC (41ºF) or less. P"
   Only change: numerals spelled out ("two", "six"). Same limits.

3. **The 6 hours are counted from 135°F, not from 70°F** (what the trap line rests on). Food Code 2022 Annex 3
   (Public Health Reasons), 3-501.14, identical in the 2026 Annex 3:
   > "An example of proper cooling might involve cooling from 135ºF to 70ºF in 1 hour, in which case 5 hours remain
   > for cooling from 70ºF to 41ºF or 45ºF."
   And Food Code 2026 Annex 7 (inspection report guide), item 20 "Proper cooling time and temperatures":
   > "the food must be cooled from 135°F to 41°F or less in 6 hours provided that the food is cooled from 135°F to
   > 70°F within the first two hours. For example, if a facility cools chili from 135°F to 70°F in 1.5 hours; they
   > then have 4.5 hours to get it from 70°F to 41°F or less."

4. **The 4-hour limit belongs to a different case** (why option A is wrong). Food Code 2022 § 3-501.14(B), same in 2026:
   > "(B) TIME/TEMPERATURE CONTROL FOR SAFETY FOOD shall be cooled within 4 hours to 5oC (41oF) or less if prepared
   > from ingredients at ambient temperature, such as reconstituted FOODS and canned tuna. P"
   Chili that has been cooked and is cooling down from 135°F is paragraph (A) food, not (B).

5. **Why the question says "Using the Food Code cooling time limits".** The 2026 edition adds an alternative in
   § 3-501.15:
   > "(A) Except as specified in ¶ (C), cooling shall be accomplished in accordance with the time and temperature
   > criteria specified under § 3-501.14 ..." and "(C) ... a *FOOD ESTABLISHMENT may cool cooked,
   > *TIME/TEMPERATURE CONTROL FOR SAFETY FOOD, if *APPROVED and: (1) Filled no more than two inches deep,
   > (2) Held uncovered, and (3) Held in a refrigeration unit that: (a) Maintains ambient temperature of 5°C (41°F)
   > or less, and (b) Is equipped with an electronic system that continuously monitors time and ambient air temperature."
   An approved (C) process is judged by that method, not the clock, so the question pins the time-limit rule.

6. **Corrective action if stage 1 is missed — NOT named in the Food Code.** Neither § 3-501.14 (2022 or 2026) nor its
   Annex 3 entry prescribes a corrective action (e.g. reheat-and-restart). Annex 3 says only: "The initial 2-hour cool
   is a critical element of this cooling process ... if cooling from 135ºF to 41°F or 45°F is achieved in 6 hours,
   but the initial cooling to 70ºF took 3 hours, the food safety hazards may not be adequately controlled."
   The film therefore makes no corrective-action claim.

## Worked example (illustrative figures)

Chili at 135°F at 2:00 PM (example time).

- Stage 1, 135°F to 70°F, within 2 hours: 2:00 PM + 2 h = 4:00 PM
- Stage 2, 135°F to 41°F or less, within a total of 6 hours (clock still starts at 2:00 PM): 2:00 PM + 6 h = 8:00 PM
  (so after reaching 70°F at 4:00 PM at the latest, at least 8:00 − 4:00 = 4 hours remain)

Answer: **B) 4:00 PM, then 8:00 PM**

Distractors, each wrong for a stated reason:
- A) 4:00 PM, then 6:00 PM — uses the 4-hour limit of ¶ (B) (ambient-temperature ingredients): 2:00 PM + 4 h = 6:00 PM.
- C) 4:00 PM, then 10:00 PM — starts the 6 hours at 70°F instead of 135°F: 4:00 PM + 6 h = 10:00 PM (8 hours total).
  This is the trap line on screen: "C) 10:00 PM restarts the clock at 70°F. The 6 hours start at 135°F."
- D) 6:00 PM, then 8:00 PM — swaps the stage lengths: 4 hours to 70°F (2:00 PM + 4 h = 6:00 PM), then 2 more
  (6:00 PM + 2 h = 8:00 PM); stage 1 is 2 hours.

Other numbers on screen: 135, 70, 41 (°F, above); 2:00 / 4:00 / 6:00 / 8:00 / 10:00 PM (above); "2 h", "6 h"
(the two limits); "stage 1", "stage 2" (labels); "3-501.14(A)" (the section); countdown ring digits 5, 4, 3, 2, 1
(a five-second pause timer, not data).

## Cold solve (builder, after writing)
Exactly one option survives: B. The start time and the rule set are written into the question; "cooked chili" puts
it under ¶ (A), not the 4-hour ¶ (B); "Using the Food Code cooling time limits" excludes the 2026 ¶ 3-501.15(C)
approved-method alternative; 2022 and 2026 editions give the same limits. A local code on the 2017 edition has the same wording — Food Code 2017 § 3-501.14(A), read via
https://web.archive.org/web/2020id_/https://www.fda.gov/media/110822/download: "(1) Within 2 hours from 57ºC (135ºF)
to 21ºC (70°F); P and (2) Within a total of 6 hours from 57ºC (135ºF) to 5ºC (41°F) or less. P" — so 2017, 2022 and
2026 all give the same answer.
