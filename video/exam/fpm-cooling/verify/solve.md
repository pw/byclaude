**Rule:** FDA Food Code §3-501.14(A) says cooked TCS food must be cooled in two stages:
1. From 135°F down to 70°F within **2 hours**.
2. From 135°F down to 41°F or less within a **total of 6 hours**. That means the second stage, 70°F to 41°F, gets the remaining 4 hours.

**Applying it:**
- The chili is at 135°F at 2:00 PM, so the 6-hour clock starts then.
- It must reach 70°F by 2:00 + 2 h = **4:00 PM**.
- It must reach 41°F by 2:00 + 6 h = **8:00 PM**.

**Why the other options are wrong:**
- **A (4 PM, then 6 PM):** gives only 2 hours for the second stage. That is a common mix-up of the two stage limits.
- **C (4 PM, then 10 PM):** gives 6 hours after reaching 70°F, for 8 hours total. It confuses "6 hours total" with "6 more hours".
- **D (6 PM, then 8 PM):** allows 4 hours for the first stage, which breaks the 2-hour limit.

**Is any other option defensible?** No.
- **Earlier cooling:** if the chili reaches 70°F before 4:00 PM, the 6-hour total still caps the 41°F deadline at 8:00 PM. The ServSafe wording ("within the next 4 hours") only moves the deadline earlier in that case, never later. Neither reading supports A, C or D.
- **Edition differences:** older codes (1999 Food Code and some legacy state codes) started the clock at 140°F. That is a moot point here because the question states the food is at 135°F at 2:00 PM. The 2005 through 2022 Food Codes all use the 135→70°F in 2 h / →41°F in 6 h total rule.

**Numbers and units:** 135°F, 70°F and 41°F match the current Food Code, and the times are consistent. There are no inconsistencies.

ANSWER: B
AMBIGUOUS: no
