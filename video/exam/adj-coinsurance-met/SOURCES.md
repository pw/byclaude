# adj-coinsurance-met — sources

Film: commercial property coinsurance where the requirement is met exactly, so no penalty applies and the insurer
pays the loss minus the deductible. The figures are made up for the example (the question card carries an `example` tag; the end card
says `Rules vary by state.`). The question is original and was not taken from any exam, vendor item bank or prep company.

## Rules and their authority

1. **The coinsurance condition: the penalty applies only if value × coinsurance % is GREATER THAN the limit; the four
   steps; the deductible is subtracted last.** Standard commercial property form language: ISO Building and Personal
   Property Coverage Form CP 00 10 06 95 (Additional Conditions, 1. Coinsurance). Copy of the form:
   https://www.proper.insure/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Loss-Conditions.pdf. The same four steps, word for word, are
   quoted from a policy in the court opinion under source 3 below.
   > "a. We will not pay the full amount of any loss if the value of Covered Property at the time of loss times the
   > Coinsurance percentage shown for it in the Declarations is greater than the Limit of Insurance for the property.
   > Instead, we will determine the most we will pay using the following steps: (1) Multiply the value of Covered
   > Property at the time of loss by the Coinsurance percentage; (2) Divide the Limit of Insurance of the property by
   > the figure determined in step (1); (3) Multiply the total amount of loss, before the application of any
   > deductible, by the figure determined in step (2); and (4) Subtract the deductible from the figure determined in
   > step (3). We will pay the amount determined in step (4) or the limit of insurance, whichever is less."

2. **The form's own example of exactly this case (limit equal to the requirement means adequate, no penalty, pay the
   loss minus the deductible).** Same form, "Example No. 2 (Adequate Insurance)":
   > "The value of the property is $250,000 / The Coinsurance percentage for it is 80% / The Limit of Insurance for it
   > is $200,000 / The Deductible is $250 / The amount of loss is $ 40,000 / The minimum amount of insurance to meet
   > your Coinsurance requirement is $200,000 ($250,000 x 80%). Therefore, the Limit of Insurance in this Example is
   > adequate and no penalty applies. We will pay no more than $39,750 ($40,000 amount of loss minus the deductible
   > of $250)."
   And the deductible condition (D. Deductible): "We will then pay the amount of loss or damage in excess of the
   Deductible, up to the applicable Limit of Insurance, after any deduction required by the Coinsurance condition".

3. **A court reading the same clause (independent of the form copy above).** Michigan Court of Appeals, *Royal
   Property Group, LLC v Prime Insurance Syndicate, Inc.*, No. 249043 (Aug 23, 2005), quoting the policy:
   https://www.courts.michigan.gov/siteassets/case-documents/uploads/opinions/final/coa/20050823_c249043_42_152o.249043.opn.coa.pdf
   > "We will not pay the full amount of any loss if the replacement cost value of Covered Property at the time of
   > loss multiplied by the 80% Coinsurance percentage shown for it in the Declarations is greater than the limit of
   > insurance for the property. … (3) multiple [sic] the total amount of loss, before the application of any
   > deductible, by the figure determined in step (2); and (4) subtract the deductible from the figure determined in
   > step (3)."
   (That policy measured value as replacement cost; ISO's base form says "value". The question gives the building's
   value at the time of loss directly, so the valuation basis does not change the answer.)

Coinsurance percentages vary by policy (80%, 90%, 100% are common). The question states its percentage, so the
answer does not depend on any default.

## Worked example (the figures are made up for the example)

Coinsurance 90% · value at time of loss $500,000 · limit $450,000 · covered loss $80,000 · deductible $2,500.

Step 1, the requirement: 500,000 × 90% = 450,000 (on screen: "needed", "90% of value", 500,000 → 450,000)
Is 450,000 greater than the 450,000 limit? No, they are equal, so the requirement is met and no penalty applies.
(on screen: "carried", "the limit", 450,000; "MET — NO PENALTY")
Step 2: 450,000 ÷ 450,000 = 1 (on screen: "ratio", "450 ÷ 450" → "1", i.e. 450,000 ÷ 450,000 in thousands)
Step 3: 80,000 × 1 = 80,000 (on screen: "loss", 80,000)
Step 4: 80,000 – 2,500 = 77,500 (on screen: "deductible" – 2,500; "insurer pays" = 77,500)
Limit check: 77,500 < 450,000, so step 4 is what the insurer pays: **C) $77,500**.

The distractors, each wrong for a stated reason:
- A) $69,500 divides the limit by the full value instead of the 90% requirement: 450,000 ÷ 500,000 = 0.9;
  80,000 × 0.9 = 72,000; 72,000 – 2,500 = 69,500. This is the trap line on screen: "A) $69,500 divides by the full $500,000.
  Divide by the 90% requirement: $450,000."
- B) $72,000 treats "90% coinsurance" as the insurer paying 90% of the loss (health-insurance style):
  80,000 × 90% = 72,000, and it also skips the deductible. (The same number results from the A) error before the
  deductible.)
- D) $80,000 gets the no-penalty result right but forgets the deductible (80,000 × 1 = 80,000).

Other numbers on screen: 90% (the coinsurance percentage); option amounts 69,500 / 72,000 / 77,500 / 80,000
(above); countdown ring digits 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 (a five-second pause timer, not data).

## Cold solve (builder, after writing)
Only C survives. The percentage, the value and the limit are all written into the question, so the answer does not
depend on the state or on which percentage a policy uses by default. With an 80% clause instead, the result would still be $77,500 (400,000 < 450,000,
no penalty), so the slate's "80% default" trap does not produce a distinct wrong answer and was dropped. Subtracting
the deductible before or after the ratio gives the same result when the ratio is 1, so the order of the steps doesn't change
this answer either.
