In 2009, a radiocarbon lab at the University of Arizona in Tucson tested four slivers of vellum from the Voynich manuscript, a book that has never been deciphered. One theory held that Wilfrid Voynich, the book dealer who bought it in 1912, had faked it himself. The four samples came from four different pages, and all four dated the skin to the early 1400s, roughly 1404 to 1438. That dates the skin, not the writing, and the ink itself was not dated. But nothing in the inks or paints was out of place for that time. A modern fake now looks unlikely. Yet no one has proved what a single word of it says.
