1864, Rapa Nui (Easter Island). A missionary wrote that wooden tablets carved with rows of tiny signs were kept in almost every house. The lines alternate direction; the reader turns the tablet around at the end of each one. In the 1860s, slave raids and disease devastated the island, and many who knew the script were taken or died. About two dozen inscribed wooden objects survive, in museums around the world. In 2024, the wood of one tablet held in Rome was radiocarbon-dated to around 1500, long before the first European ships arrived in 1722. Part of one text may record a lunar calendar. No proposed reading of the script has ever been accepted.
