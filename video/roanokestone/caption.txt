2012, the British Museum, London. Researchers lit a map from beneath. It was drawn more than four centuries ago by John White, governor of the Roanoke colony. Under a small paper patch was a hidden fort symbol in bright red and blue, where the Roanoke and Chowan rivers meet. In 1590, White had found his colony deserted, with CROATOAN carved on a post. He wrote that the settlers had planned to move fifty miles inland. Digs near that spot have found a few English pottery sherds. Why the symbol was covered is unknown, and where the colonists went has never been proved.
