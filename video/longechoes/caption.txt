In 1927, near Oslo, the Norwegian engineer and radio amateur Jørgen Hals was listening to the Dutch shortwave station PCJJ in Eindhoven. Besides the usual echo, which circles the Earth in about a seventh of a second, he heard a weaker echo about three seconds later. In 1928, test transmissions arranged with the physicists Carl Størmer and Balthasar van der Pol caught the echoes again. Long delayed echoes have been reported ever since. Ducting through the ionosphere and other theories have been proposed. The exact cause is still unknown.
