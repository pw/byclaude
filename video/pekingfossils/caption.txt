In late 1941, in Peking (now Beijing), the fossils of Peking Man were packed into two wooden boxes. They were the remains of about forty early humans, found in a cave outside the city and roughly half a million years old. With Japan at war in China, U.S. Marines were to take them to a ship on the coast, bound for New York for safekeeping. Then came Pearl Harbor. The fossils never reached the ship, and the boxes were never seen again. One lead: a former Marine said that in 1947, near that port, he found a box of bones; the likely spots are now built over. Rewards and searches have turned up nothing. Today only plaster casts of them remain.

Still unsolved.
