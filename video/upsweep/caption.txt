August 1991, the Pacific Ocean. When NOAA began recording the Navy's undersea hydrophones, one sound was already there. A long train of rising tones, each several seconds long, loud enough to be recorded across the Pacific. It became known as Upsweep, and in the recordings it seems seasonal, peaking in spring and autumn. Its source lies roughly in the remote South Pacific, between New Zealand and South America. Whales were an early guess, but the tone barely varies. It lies near what may be undersea volcanic activity, and it has grown fainter since 1991. NOAA still calls its origin unresolved.
