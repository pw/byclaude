February 1855, south Devon, England. After a night of heavy snow, people woke to find lines of small hoof-shaped prints, about four inches long, mostly in single file. They were reported in more than 30 places, on both sides of the Exe estuary, and reports said they crossed rooftops and high walls. Some said the Devil had walked, and it was spoken of from the pulpit. One letter at the time claimed a trail ran over a hundred miles in a single night; the farthest anyone is known to have followed one was about five miles. Donkeys, wood mice, a runaway balloon and a hoax have all been suggested. None has ever explained every mark.

Still unsolved.
