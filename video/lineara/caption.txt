Around 1900, on Crete, Arthur Evans's excavations found clay tablets in a script he named Linear A. Its sister script, Linear B, used many of the same signs. In 1952 Michael Ventris deciphered Linear B. It was Greek. Read with the same sound values, Linear A's words can be spoken aloud, but they match no language scholars can understand. About 1,400 inscriptions survive. Decipherments are still claimed. None has been accepted.
