January 29, 1948, the Azores. The British airliner Star Tiger set out across the Atlantic for Bermuda, planning to fly the whole way just 2,000 feet above the sea, below the cloud and out of the worst winds. Every hourly report she sent gave her height as 20,000 feet; the inquiry called it an error for 2,000. An hour ahead, a second airliner from the same airline found the wind far stronger than forecast and was blown more than 60 miles off course in a single hour. It landed safely in Bermuda. The Star Tiger, an hour behind and blown off course too, was never seen again. The inquiry could not say how high she was flying when her radio went silent, and the wreck has never been found.

Still unsolved.
