December 1872, the Atlantic between the Azores and Portugal. The Mary Celeste was found drifting with no one aboard and 1,701 barrels of alcohol in her hold. When she was unloaded in Genoa, nine barrels were empty. The rest were white oak; those nine were red oak, a more porous wood. One theory is that vapour from the leaks filled the hold, and a sudden flash frightened all ten aboard into the ship's only boat. In 2006 a chemist tested the idea in a model hold: a wave of flame swept through, and nothing was scorched. In 2026, chemists in Manchester tried it with wood and ethanol, and again the blast left no sign of damage. It shows what could have happened, not what did.

Still unsolved.
