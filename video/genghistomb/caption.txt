In 1227, Genghis Khan died and was buried in secret in Mongolia. About eighty years later, the Persian historian Rashid al-Din wrote that the Khan had chosen the spot himself, beneath a lone tree. A forest grew there so thick that no one could tell which tree it was, and even its guardians could no longer find the way. The land around his sacred mountain, Burkhan Khaldun, became the Great Taboo, closed to outsiders for seven centuries. Stories of trampling horses and a diverted river are legends. In 2010, ten thousand volunteers searched satellite images and found 55 ancient sites. Not his grave.

Still unsolved.
