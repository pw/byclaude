June 1819, the mouth of the Singapore River. Workers clearing the jungle found a great sandstone boulder, about 3 metres tall, split into two halves that faced each other. It carried about 50 lines of carved script. In 1843, the acting settlement engineer had it blown up, to widen the river mouth and make room for a fort. A few fragments were saved and sent to Calcutta. One came back in 1918, and it is now in the National Museum of Singapore. Scholars have proposed scripts, languages and dates. The inscription has never been fully deciphered.
