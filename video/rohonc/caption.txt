In 1838, in Hungary, Count Gusztáv Batthyány gave his library to the Hungarian Academy of Sciences. In it was a small book no one could read. The Rohonc Codex: 448 pages in a script unlike any known alphabet, with 87 drawings of crosses, crescents, suns and what looks like a gospel story. Its paper was probably made in Venice around 1530. For a century, many scholars dismissed it as a forgery; that was never proven. In 2018, two researchers proposed a partial reading, as Christian texts written in a code. No complete reading has ever been accepted.

Still unsolved.
