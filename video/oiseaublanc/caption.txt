May 8, 1927, Le Bourget, Paris. Twelve days before Lindbergh, Charles Nungesser and François Coli took off in a white biplane, L'Oiseau Blanc, to fly non-stop to New York. Soon after take-off they dropped the landing gear to save weight; they meant to land on the water at New York. The plane was seen leaving the French coast. In Paris, a newspaper reported that they had arrived. They never did. People in Newfoundland and Maine later reported hearing an engine, but none of it was ever confirmed. Decades of searches have found no confirmed wreckage. The only piece known is the landing gear they dropped, now in the museum at Le Bourget.

Still unsolved.
