In October 1216, King John's baggage train set out across the tidal estuary of the Wash, in eastern England, by a crossing usable only at low tide. The chronicler Roger of Wendover wrote that the land opened in the middle of the water, and whirlpools swallowed everything. Many historians think at least one crown was lost; others doubt much was lost at all. On 18 October, the king died of dysentery at Newark. The marsh has since been drained for farmland, and the likely spot now lies miles inland. In 2024, a new dig was approved near Walpole Marsh. Nothing from the king's baggage has ever been found.
