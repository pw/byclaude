## Review: "nobody sent me a 1099" (tax-no-form)

**Arithmetic:** 6,200 + 1,500 + 9,500 + 14,800 = 32,000. The sum is correct.

**Claims that hold up against IRS.gov:**
- **"Business income is taxable, form or no form" / "If you earned it, it counts."** IRS: *"Whether or not you receive a Form 1099-K, you must still report any income on your tax return."* ([Understanding your Form 1099-K](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/understanding-your-form-1099-k))
- **Card reader → "1099-K".** IRS: *"If your customers or clients pay you directly by credit, debit or gift card, you'll get a Form 1099-K from your payment card processor no matter how many payments you got or how much they were for."* (same page)
- **Payment app $9,500 → "maybe a 1099-K".** The federal threshold is *"exceeds $20,000 in more than 200 transactions"* (same page). $9,500 is under it, so the app doesn't have to send one federally but may anyway. "Maybe" is the right word.
- **"Your regular clients usually don't file one at all."** IRS: *"Report on Form 1099-MISC or Form 1099-NEC only when payments are made in the course of your trade or business. Personal payments are not reportable."* ([i1099mec](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec)) That's true for people whose clients are individuals (stylists, groomers). See nit 2 for tradespeople.
- **"every payment in one place, as it happens."** This is general record-keeping advice and matches IRS guidance to report gross receipts from your books and records ([Schedule C instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sc)). It doesn't tell any one person what to do.
- The worked numbers are labelled "example", and there's a states caveat and a disclaimer.

### Findings

**1. MISLEADING: "income / 32,000 / all of it counts", followed straight away by "Business income is taxable, form or no form."**
Read in that order, a booth renter will take it to mean they're taxed on the full $32,000. They're not. The $32,000 of gross receipts all has to be *reported*, but tax is figured on net profit after expenses like booth rent and supplies. For this audience that gap can be many thousands of dollars.
IRS: *"you must figure any net profit or net loss from your business. You do this by subtracting your business expenses from your business income."* and *"You have to file an income tax return if your net earnings from self-employment were $400 or more."* ([Self-Employed Individuals Tax Center](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center))
**Replace with:** "32,000 in sales" / "all of it gets reported" / "(business expenses come off before tax)"

**2. ORDER/RENDER CHECK: four amounts but only three form labels ("no form", "maybe a 1099-K", "1099-K").**
If "checks 1,500" shows no label, or the labels shift up a row, a viewer could pair "maybe a 1099-K" with checks or "1099-K" with the payment app. The payment-app pairing would contradict the $20,000 threshold above.
**Fix:** check the frame and make sure each row carries its own label: cash = "no form", checks = "no form", payment app = "maybe a 1099-K", card reader = "1099-K".

**Nit 1 (not blocking): "A report someone else files about money they paid you."**
A 1099-K is filed by the card processor or payment app, not by whoever paid you.
**Suggested:** "A report someone else files about money paid to you."

**Nit 2 (not blocking): "Your regular clients usually don't file one at all."**
Tradespeople with business clients may get a 1099-NEC. The threshold is $2,000 for tax years beginning after 2025 (i1099mec). The word "usually" covers it.

The caption is accurate as written.
