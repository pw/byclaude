**What checks out**

- **Arithmetic:** 48,000 − 12,000 − 3,000 − 600 = 32,400. Correct. The "example" label comes before the numbers, which is the right place for it.
- **"net profit · Schedule C, line 31":** Correct. The Schedule C instructions (2025, https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040sc) say: *"Individuals. Enter your net profit or loss on line 31 and include it on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 3. Also, include your net profit or loss on Schedule SE (Form 1040), line 2."*
- **"gross receipts":** This is the IRS term for line 1. Same page: *"Line 1 Enter gross receipts from your trade or business."*
- **"Your federal tax starts from profit — not from everything that came in.":** Accurate. Both income tax and self-employment (SE) tax start from line 31. "Starts from" is the right hedge, because more adjustments follow (SE tax is figured on 92.35% of net earnings, and there's the standard deduction and others). It does not claim the viewer is taxed on the full 32,400.
- **Booth rent and supplies:** Both are deductible. The same instructions cover line 20b (*"amounts paid to rent or lease other property"*) and line 22 (*"You can deduct the cost of materials and supplies only to the extent you actually consumed and used them in your business"*).
- **"Business part only":** Publication 334 (2025, https://www.irs.gov/publications/p334) says: *"If you have an expense that is partly for business and partly personal, separate the personal part from the business part. The personal part is generally not deductible."*
- **"Deductible business costs":** "Deductible" carries the ordinary-and-necessary test well enough for a short video. Pub 334: *"To be deductible, a business expense must be both ordinary and necessary."*

**Nits (they don't mislead a viewer)**

1. **"skills class / 600"**: A class is only deductible if it keeps up or improves skills for the current trade. Publication 970 (https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970) says education *"isn't qualifying work-related education if it: Is needed to meet the minimum educational requirements of your present trade or business, or Is part of a program of study that will qualify you for a new trade or business."* In a stylist's example, a skills class fits the rule, and the line is labelled "example". If you want the condition on screen, a possible replacement is **"skills class (same trade)"**.
2. **"Forget one, and it stays in profit — taxed as if you kept it."**: This is true of the return as filed. A missed expense can often be claimed later on an amended return (Form 1040-X). That sentence was not pulled from IRS.gov in this review. The line doesn't tell anyone to do anything wrong, so it's fine as written. An optional softer version is "Forget one, and it stays in profit — taxed as if you kept it, unless you amend."
