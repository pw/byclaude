# Review: "made 48k" / tax-profit explainer

**Arithmetic:** 48,000 − 12,000 − 3,000 − 600 = 32,400. Correct. The numbers are labelled "example" right after THE MATH, so they read as illustrative.

**Claims that check out:**
- **"Your federal tax starts from profit — not from everything that came in."** This holds. The IRS self-employed tax center says: *"Self-employed individuals generally must pay self-employment (SE) tax as well as income tax"* and *"You do this by subtracting your business expenses from your business income."* The SE tax page says: *"you generally use Schedule C to figure net earnings from self-employment."* "Starts from" is the right verb, because the profit figure is a starting point, not the tax. (https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center, https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes)
- **"net profit · Schedule C, line 31"** is correct. Line 31 is Net profit or (loss).
- **"booth rent"** is deductible as rent of business property. The Schedule C instructions for line 20b say: *"amounts paid to rent or lease other property, such as office space in a building."*
- **"color + supplies"** is fine for supplies used up in the business. The line 22 instruction says: *"deduct the cost of materials and supplies only to the extent you actually consumed and used them in your business."*
- **"skills class"** is fine as an example. Pub 970 allows education that *"maintain[s] or improve[s] skills needed in [your] present work"* but not education that *"qualifies you for a new trade or business."* The label "skills class" fits the allowed case.
- **"Business part only."** is right. The IRS FAQ says: *"You must divide these expenses between business and personal use."* (https://www.irs.gov/faqs/small-business-self-employed-other-business/income-expenses)
- **"Forget one, and it stays in profit — taxed as if you kept it."** and **"The deductible ones come off before the tax is figured."** are both accurate.

**Order:** The "the one you forgot ?" row appears after the profit total. That works: it shows an expense that never got subtracted, and the next line explains it. It doesn't mislead.

## Finding (1)

**On-screen string:** "Business costs you can prove come off the top. Business part only."

**What's wrong:** This is a universal claim missing its condition. Proof is the record-keeping requirement, not the test for whether something is deductible. The test is "ordinary and necessary." Without it, the line tells viewers that any business cost with a receipt comes off. That matters for this audience because common stylist and trades misconceptions fail the test even with a receipt:
- everyday-wearable work clothes
- a class that qualifies you for a new trade
- penalties

The closing line ("The deductible ones…") quietly concedes that not every cost qualifies, so the two lines contradict each other.

**IRS source:** https://itap1.for.irs.gov/owda/0/resource/Commentary_Files_Redirect_ITA/en-US/help/ordnec.html (quoting Pub 535): *"An ordinary expense is one that is common and accepted in your industry." / "A necessary expense is one that is helpful and appropriate for your trade or business."* The records page shows proof is a separate requirement: *"Your supporting documents should identify the payee, the amount paid, proof of payment, the date incurred, and include a description of the item purchased or service received."* (https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/what-kind-of-records-should-i-keep)

**Suggested replacement:** "Ordinary, necessary business costs come off the top — if you can prove them. Business part only."

No other findings. The fix is one line; everything else is accurate and correctly hedged.
