I checked every claim against IRS Publication 505 (2026), https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505. The rates, thresholds, the zero-tax conditions and all the arithmetic hold up. Two things could mislead someone who acts on the video.

**Finding 1: "tax on last year's return" / "100% of the tax on last year's return" (would mislead)**
- **What's wrong:** A self-employed viewer who opens last year's Form 1040 will find a line simply called "Tax" (line 16). That line leaves out self-employment tax, which is often the biggest part of a stylist's or groomer's bill. The safe-harbor figure is the *total* tax on line 24, and line 24 includes self-employment tax. Someone who pays 100% of line 16 could fall short and still owe the penalty. The worked example ("tax on last year's return 4,000") has the same gap.
- **IRS quote (Pub 505, Required Annual Payment):** "The total amount you must pay is the smaller of: 90% of your total expected tax for 2026, or 100% of the total tax shown on your 2025 return." And under "Total tax for 2025—line 12b": "Your 2025 total tax is the amount on Form 1040 or 1040–SR, line 24 reduced by the following…" (the reductions include refundable credits). The same publication says: "Estimated tax is used to pay not only income tax but other taxes such as self-employment tax."
- **Replacement:** "100% of the TOTAL tax on last year's return (includes self-employment tax)". In the example: "total tax on last year's return (example)".

**Finding 2: "each quarter, on time" → "Safe from the penalty, generally." (would mislead a viewer who sees it now)**
- **What's wrong:** Today is 2026-09-26, and three of the four 2026 due dates have already passed (April 15, June 15, Sept 15). A viewer asking Tash's question now could fairly conclude "I'll pay last year's tax now and I'm safe." That isn't true: the penalty is figured separately for each payment period. The example itself is correct as worded, but the video never says what happens if you missed a date.
- **IRS quote (Pub 505):** "The penalty is figured separately for each payment period." And: "If you don't pay enough tax by the due date of each of the payment periods, you may be charged a penalty even if you are due a refund." The 2026 dates are listed as "April 15, 2026 … June 15, 2026 … Sept. 15, 2026 … Jan. 15, 2027".
- **Replacement:** add a short card after "Not from the bill.": "Missed a due date? The penalty is figured per payment. Paying late still keeps it smaller." Or add the dates: "Due dates: Apr 15 · Jun 15 · Sep 15 · Jan 15".

**Minor points (wording, not misleading on their own)**
- **"four due dates ÷ 4 … each quarter":** the payment periods aren't equal quarters (Jan–Mar, Apr–May, Jun–Aug, Sep–Dec). "each due date" is more accurate. Four equal payments is still right: Pub 505 says "You must usually pay this difference in four equal installments."
- **The 12-month condition:** Pub 505 also requires "Your 2025 tax return must cover all 12 months." The video shows this only on the zero-tax card. It almost never matters for a calendar-year individual, so leaving it out is acceptable.
- **The under-$1,000 exception isn't mentioned.** Pub 505 says you don't have to pay estimated tax if what you'll owe after withholding "is less than $1,000." "Generally" covers leaving this out.

**Checked and correct**
- "Smaller of 90% / 100%" is correct.
- The 110% rule (AGI over $150,000, or $75,000 married filing separately) is correct and still current for 2026. Pub 505: "If your AGI for 2025 was more than $150,000 ($75,000 if your filing status for 2026 is Married filing separately), substitute 110%…" The exception for farming and fishing income doesn't matter for this audience.
- The zero-tax card matches Pub 505 word for word: "You had no tax liability for 2025. You were a U.S. citizen or resident alien for the whole year. Your 2025 tax year covered a 12-month period… if your total tax… was zero or you didn't have to file." "A refund doesn't mean $0" is correct.
- Arithmetic: 4,000 ÷ 4 = 1,000, and 6,000 − 4,000 = 2,000. Both are labelled "example".
- The caption is accurate and hedged.
- The order of the cards is fine. The penalty line comes after "on time", and the "Not from the bill" warning comes right after it.
