## What I checked and found correct

- **"Generally, if you expect to owe $1,000 or more when you file."** Correct. irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes: *"Individuals, including sole proprietors… generally have to make estimated tax payments if they expect to owe tax of $1,000 or more when their return is filed."*
- **The four due dates and the months each one covers.** The 2026 Form 1040-ES (irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f1040es.pdf) lists *"1st payment… April 15, 2026 / 2nd… June 15, 2026 / 3rd… Sept. 15, 2026 / 4th… Jan. 15, 2027"*. The estimated-tax FAQ gives the periods as *"Jan. 1–March 31 / April 1–May 31 / June 1–Aug. 31 / Sept. 1–Dec. 31."* Each date lines up with the right months. The uneven split (a 2-month quarter and a 4-month quarter) is shown correctly.
- **"Miss one, or pay too little? You may owe a penalty."** Correct, and "may" is the right word because there are exceptions. The FAQ says a penalty can apply *"even if you're due a refund."*
- **"Form 1040-ES" and "pay at IRS.gov/payments."** Correct. The Estimated Taxes page lists IRS.gov/payments, Direct Pay and EFTPS as ways to pay.
- **Caption.** Correct, including "For 2026 income the last date is January 15, 2027."
- **Arithmetic.** There's no worked example and no illustrative dollar figure, so nothing needs an "example" label.

## Findings

**1. "× the IRS quarterly rate"**
- **Problem:** A viewer can read this as a rate charged per quarter. The IRS sets an annual rate every quarter (currently 7%) and charges it by the day.
- **Source:** The underpayment-penalty page (irs.gov/payments/underpayment-of-estimated-tax-by-individuals-penalty) bases the penalty on *"The published quarterly interest rates for underpayments."* The Q4 2026 release says *"7% for underpayments… the rate of interest is determined on a quarterly basis."*
- **Replace with:** `× the IRS interest rate (reset each quarter)`

**2. "Set some aside every week. / Send it four times a year."**
- **Problem:** This reads as an instruction to every viewer, but not everyone has to pay estimated tax. People who expect to owe under $1,000 don't. For the fourth payment, the 1040-ES says: *"You don't have to make the payment due January 15, 2027, if you file your 2026 tax return by February 1, 2027, and pay the entire balance due with your return."*
- **Replace with:** `One common habit: set some aside every week, pay by each due date.`

**3. Gap in coverage: self-employment tax (not a false statement)**
- **Problem:** The post says "federal" and contrasts a job with a booth. It never says that estimated payments also cover Social Security and Medicare (self-employment tax). Forgetting that part is the most common reason people in this audience underpay.
- **Source:** The Estimated Taxes page says estimated payments cover *"income tax, self-employment tax, and alternative minimum tax."*
- **Suggested add** (after "pay as you go"): `That includes self-employment tax (Social Security + Medicare).`

**4. Order: "THE MATH" heading**
- **Problem:** The frames right after this heading are the job-vs-booth comparison, not any math. This is a style issue only.
- **Replace with:** `WHY`, or drop the heading.

**5. Timing (posted 26 Sept 2026)**
- **Problem:** Three of the four 2026 dates have already passed. The "next" badge must sit on Jan. 15, 2027. In the text order above it comes after the list, so I couldn't tell which date it labels. Check the frame visually.
- **Optional add:** `Missed one? Paying sooner stops the clock.` This is accurate because the penalty is figured on the number of days late.

**6. Caption spelling (nit)**
- "instalments" is British spelling. Use `installments` for a US audience.
