**Review: the estimated-tax video for booth renters.** I checked each claim against IRS.gov: Pub 505 (2026), the estimated-taxes page, the underpayment-penalty page, and the Form 2210 instructions. The only numbers in the video are the $1,000 threshold and the dates, so there's no arithmetic to check and nothing that needs an "example" label.

**Confirmed accurate**
- **"pay as you go"**: IRS says *"Taxes must be paid as you earn or receive income during the year, either through withholding or estimated tax payments."* (irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes)
- **"covers income tax and self-employment tax"**: *"Estimated tax is used to pay not only income tax, but other taxes such as self-employment tax and alternative minimum tax."* (same page)
- **"Generally, if you expect to owe $1,000 or more when you file"**: the IRS page says individuals must pay if they *"expect to owe tax of $1,000 or more when their return is filed."*
- **The four dates and their periods**: Pub 505 lists *"Jan. 1 – March 31 … April 15, 2026 / April 1 – May 31 … June 15, 2026 / June 1 – Aug. 31 … Sept. 15, 2026 / Sept. 1 – Dec. 31 … Jan. 15, 2027."* None of the four falls on a weekend. (irs.gov/publications/p505)
- **The penalty is "figured like interest"** (amount short × days late × IRS rate): the IRS penalty page bases it on *"The amount of the underpayment," "The period when the underpayment was due and underpaid," and "The published quarterly interest rates for underpayments."* The 2210 worksheet computes it as *"Underpayment … × Number of days … /365 × [rate]."*
- **"The days stop counting once you pay"**: the 2210 instructions say *"The penalty is figured for the number of days that each underpayment remains unpaid."*
- **Form 1040-ES and IRS.gov/payments**: both are listed as payment methods on the estimated-taxes page.
- **Caption**: accurate.

**Nits (none would mislead a viewer)**
1. **The $1,000 line**: the full rule is *"$1000 or more … after subtracting income tax withholding and refundable credits"* (Pub 505, Figure 2-A). "Generally" covers it, and for someone with only booth income the two are nearly the same. A stylist who also has a W-2 job is the one case where it matters. Optional replacement: "Generally, if you expect to owe $1,000 or more when you file, after any withholding."
2. **"You may owe a penalty"**: this is correct. The IRS adds that you can owe it *"even if you are due a refund when you file your tax return"* (Pub 505). That's worth adding if there's room, but it isn't required.
3. **"The days stop counting once you pay"**: true, with one catch. The IRS applies payments *"first to any underpayment balance on an earlier installment even if you designate a payment for a later period"* (i2210). A late payment clears the oldest shortfall first. It's fine as written.
4. **The Jan. 15, 2027 payment**: it's optional if you *"file your 2026 Form 1040 or 1040-SR by January 31, 2027, and pay the rest of the tax you owe"* (Pub 505). Leaving this out doesn't mislead, because Jan. 15 is still a correct due date.
5. **Order and timing**: today is Sept. 26, 2026, so the next due date is Jan. 15, 2027. Make sure the "next" marker points at that row (Jan. 15, 2027 / Sep – Dec). If it points at any other row, it's wrong. Also, a viewer seeing this now has already passed three of the four dates. The "Missed one? The days stop counting once you pay" card handles that well, so keep it right after the dates.
6. **"THE MATH"**: the header sits over a job-versus-booth comparison, not over any math. That's a style point only.
