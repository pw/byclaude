## Review: federal estimated tax explainer (booth renters)

I checked every rule, date, form name and link on screen against IRS.gov and found no false statements. The four due dates (April 15, 2026 is a Wednesday, June 15 a Monday, Sept 15 a Tuesday, Jan 15, 2027 a Friday) and their payment periods are correct, and none falls on a weekend or holiday. The $1,000 threshold, how the penalty is figured, Form 1040-ES and IRS.gov/payments are all correct. The problem is the order of the closing lines.

### Finding 1: the closing lines are in the wrong order and read as a promise (**must fix**)
**On screen:** `Set some aside every week.` / `Pay by each due date.` / `One common habit:` / `That covers income tax and self-employment tax.`

**What's wrong:**
- "One common habit:" appears *after* the habit it introduces, and nothing follows it.
- The next line, "That covers income tax and self-employment tax," then attaches to "set some aside every week."
- A viewer can read it as: setting aside *some* money (any amount) covers both taxes. That isn't true. The IRS point is that **estimated tax payments** are the way both taxes get paid. Whether the payments are enough depends on how much is paid.
- If a graphic between these lines shows a percentage, it isn't in the text, so I couldn't check it. Any percentage would need an "example" label.

**Source:** https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes. It says "Estimated tax is used to pay not only income tax, but other taxes such as self-employment tax and alternative minimum tax."

**Suggested replacement (in this order):**
`One common habit:` → `Set some aside every week.` → `Pay by each due date.` → `Your estimated payments cover income tax and self-employment tax.`

### Finding 2: three of the four dates have already passed (optional)
**On screen:** `4 due dates for 2026 income` … `next`

**What's wrong:** If this posts now (late September 2026), three of the four 2026 due dates are already gone. Someone self-employed who has paid nothing yet may read "next" as "I'm just starting on time." The penalty is figured separately for each period, from that period's due date. Nothing on screen is false, and the "× how many days late" line hints at this.

**Source:** https://www.irs.gov/payments/underpayment-of-estimated-tax-by-individuals-penalty. It says the penalty is based on "The period when the underpayment was due and underpaid."

**Suggested addition (optional):** `Late payments still count. The penalty is figured separately for each missed date.`

### Finding 3: the $1,000 threshold leaves out a condition (nit)
**On screen:** `Generally, if you expect to owe $1,000 or more when you file.`

**What's wrong:** The IRS threshold is measured *after* withholding and credits. This audience usually has no withholding, so the missing condition barely matters. The line is accurate as written.

**Source:** https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes. It says people generally need to pay estimated tax if they "expect to owe tax of $1,000 or more when their return is filed." The same page says you generally avoid the penalty if you owe "less than $1,000 after credits/withholdings."

**Suggested replacement (optional):** `Generally, if you expect to owe $1,000 or more (after withholding and credits) when you file.`

### Finding 4: "THE MATH" heading (nit)
**On screen:** `THE MATH`

**What's wrong:** That frame is a comparison between a job and a booth, not a calculation.

**Suggested replacement:** `THE DIFFERENCE`

### Checked and correct
- **Penalty wording:** "may owe a penalty" is appropriately hedged. People who meet the 90% (this year) or 100% (last year) payment rules owe no penalty.
- **Penalty formula:** "how much was short × how many days late × the IRS interest rate" matches the IRS's three factors (https://www.irs.gov/payments/underpayment-of-estimated-tax-by-individuals-penalty).
- **Worked examples:** there are no dollar calculations on screen, so there was no arithmetic to check.
- **Labelling:** the on-screen text doesn't tell any specific person what to do. The state-tax caveat and the "General information, not tax advice" line are both present.
- **Caption:** accurate.
