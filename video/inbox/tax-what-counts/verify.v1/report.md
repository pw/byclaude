I checked every claim against the IRS publications for 2025 returns (Pub 334, Pub 463, Pub 970 and Pub 583, pulled directly from irs.gov). The video itself is accurate. One finding would mislead part of the audience, and it's in the caption.

## Finding 1: needs a fix

**String (caption):** "Lunch by yourself, the drive to work, and the gym usually don't."

**What's wrong:** On screen, the claim is limited to "the drive from home to your booth", which is correct. The caption widens it to "the drive to work". This video is aimed at groomers and tradespeople too, and for them the rule is often the opposite. If their home office counts as their main place of business (common for someone who does their admin and scheduling at home), the drive from home to a client's house or job site can be deducted. "Usually don't" tells that group they can't deduct something they can.

**IRS source:** Pub 463, https://www.irs.gov/publications/p463:
> "If you have an office in your home that qualifies as a principal place of business, you can deduct your daily transportation costs between your home and another work location in the same trade or business."

and
> "(2) your residence is your principal place of business and you incur expenses going between the residence and another work location in the same trade or business, regardless of whether the work is temporary or permanent and regardless of the distance."

**Replacement:** "Lunch by yourself, the drive from home to your booth, and the gym usually don't."

## Minor points (no fix needed)

- **"shears + supplies / tools of the trade"** is correct. Pricier equipment sometimes has to be written off over several years instead of all at once, but it still counts as a business expense. Pub 334 lists "Supplies and materials" as deductible and points to Pub 946 for depreciation.
- **"the drive from home to your booth / commuting, usually"** is correct, and "usually" covers the home-office exception above. Pub 463: "You can't deduct commuting expenses no matter how far your home is from your regular place of work."
- **"THE MATH"** is a header over a screen with no numbers. It's a style issue only. Since there are no figures anywhere, no "example" labels are needed.

## Checked and correct

- **ordinary / necessary.** Pub 334: "An ordinary expense is one that is common and accepted in your field of business. A necessary expense is one that is helpful and appropriate for your business."
- **booth rent.** Pub 334: "you can deduct rent as a business expense only if the rent is for property you use in your business."
- **a class that keeps your current skills sharp, generally.** Pub 970: education qualifies "only if it maintains or improves skills needed in your present work." The words "current" and "generally" cover the rule that a class qualifying you for a new trade doesn't count.
- **your phone, the business share only.** Pub 334: "If you have an expense that is partly for business and partly personal, separate the personal part from the business part. The personal part is generally not deductible."
- **lunch by yourself, on a normal workday.** Pub 463: "You can deduct a portion of the cost of meals if it is necessary for you to stop for substantial sleep or rest to properly perform your duties while traveling away from home on business." The words "on a normal workday" correctly leave out overnight travel.
- **your gym, personal.** Pub 334, "Expenses You Cannot Deduct": "Dues to business, social, athletic, luncheon, sporting, airline, and hotel clubs … Personal, living, and family expenses."
- **keep the receipt and a note of what it was for.** Pub 583 says supporting documents "include sales slips, paid bills, invoices, receipts…". Pub 463 says to record the business purpose.
- **Order:** the opening message asks about lunch, and lunch lands in the DOESN'T column, so the sequence answers the question honestly. The closing lines don't give anyone individual instructions.
