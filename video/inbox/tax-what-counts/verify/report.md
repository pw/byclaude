# Review: "what counts as a business expense" reel

I checked every rule on screen and in the caption against the current IRS publications: Pub 334 (2025), Pub 463, Pub 587, Pub 583 and Tax Topic 513. I pulled the pages as raw HTML and matched the quotes below word for word, rather than relying on a summary. Nothing on screen or in the caption is false, out of date, or would lead a viewer into a wrong deduction. There are no worked numbers, so there was no arithmetic to check and nothing needed an "example" label.

## Claims that hold up

| On-screen string | IRS source (verbatim) |
|---|---|
| "ordinary / common and accepted in your line of work" · "necessary / helpful and appropriate for it" | Pub 334: *"An ordinary expense is one that is common and accepted in your field of business. A necessary expense is one that is helpful and appropriate for your business."* |
| "booth rent" | Pub 334: *"Generally, rent paid in your business is deductible in the year paid or accrued."* |
| "a class that keeps your current skills sharp / generally" | Tax Topic 513: education that *"maintains or improves skills needed in your present work"*, but not if it is *"part of a program that will qualify you for a new trade or business."* The word "current" plus "generally" covers that exception. |
| "your phone / the business share only" | Pub 334: *"If you have an expense that is partly for business and partly personal, separate the personal part from the business part. The personal part is generally not deductible."* |
| "the drive from home to your booth / commuting, usually" | Pub 463: *"Daily transportation expenses you incur while traveling from home to one or more regular places of business are generally nondeductible commuting expenses."* The home-office exception exists (*"If you have an office in your home that qualifies as a principal place of business, you can deduct your daily transportation costs between your home and another work location…"*), and "usually" covers it. |
| "lunch by yourself / personal, on a normal workday" | Pub 463: meals are deductible when *"traveling away from home"* overnight, or as business meals *"if you (or an employee) is present"* with a business contact. A solo lunch at home base fits neither case. |
| "your gym / personal" | Personal expense, not deductible, under the same Pub 334 rule as the phone row. |
| "keep the proof / the receipt / and a note of what it was for" | Pub 583: *"Supporting documents include sales slips, paid bills, invoices, receipts…"* Pub 463: *"…adequate if it shows the amount, date, place, and essential character of the expense."* |

## Nits (none would mislead a viewer)

1. **"THE MATH"**: the reel contains no math. It reads like a leftover template header.
   - Suggested replacement: "THE RULE" or "THE TEST".
2. **"the IRS test"** makes "ordinary + necessary" sound like the whole test. An expense must also be for the business and not personal, which the DOESN'T column shows, so this is only a nit.
   - Optional replacement: "the IRS starting test".
3. **"keep the proof"** is enough for booth rent and supplies. Meals and travel need more records under Pub 463 (date, place, business purpose, and for meals the business relationship). The reel never tells anyone a meal counts, so this can't cause a bad deduction.
4. **Order**: the rule comes before the examples, and "If it's for the business, keep the receipt" comes after the counts/doesn't columns. That order doesn't mislead.

Nothing on screen tells a particular person what to do. The disclaimer is present both on screen and in the caption. The caption matches the on-screen verdicts, including the hedging ("usually count" / "usually don't").
