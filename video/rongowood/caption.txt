2024. Scientists radiocarbon-dated four rongorongo tablets from Rapa Nui (Easter Island), kept in Rome. The wood of one, a notched tablet called Échancrée, came from a tree felled around 1500, more than two centuries before Europeans arrived in 1722. If the writing is that old, the islanders may have invented it on their own, as only a handful of places in history ever have. But dating the wood is not dating the carving, and the study's authors say so. That wood never grew on the island; it may have drifted ashore or come off a European ship. Some linguists question whether the script is full writing at all. Fewer than 30 inscribed objects survive, and the script has never been deciphered.
