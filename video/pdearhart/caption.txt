July 2, 1937, the central Pacific. Amelia Earhart and her navigator, Fred Noonan, took off from Lae, New Guinea, in their Lockheed Electra, bound for tiny Howland Island. The Coast Guard cutter Itasca waited offshore, and its radio log recorded her voice: "We must be on you but cannot see you." They never landed. The search that followed, the costliest the US had mounted to that time, found no trace of them or the plane. Some believe the fuel ran out over the sea; others, that they reached Gardner Island, now Nikumaroro. In October 2026, an expedition there from Purdue University and the Archaeological Legacy Institute found no conclusive evidence of the plane. The images are period photographs and modern photographs of Howland and Nikumaroro, all public domain.
