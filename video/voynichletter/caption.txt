Prague, 1665 or 1666. The scholar Johannes Marcus Marci sent a book no one could read to Athanasius Kircher in Rome, the man who claimed to have deciphered Egyptian hieroglyphs. His letter is still with the book, now known as the Voynich manuscript. It says no one but Kircher could read it. It says Emperor Rudolf II was said to have paid 600 ducats for it, and that it was thought to be the work of Roger Bacon. Marci himself would not vouch for that. The book's previous owner had written to Kircher too, and tried to read it until he died. No reading from Kircher is on record.

Still unsolved.
