September 1979. An American satellite recorded a double flash over the southern Indian Ocean. Hours later, the Arecibo radio telescope in Puerto Rico picked up a ripple in the upper atmosphere, moving in an unusual direction. Underwater sound recordings were later linked by a Navy laboratory study to a blast at sea. In October and November, iodine-131, a product of nuclear fission, turned up in the thyroids of sheep in southeastern Australia. In 1980, a panel convened for the White House judged the flash probably not a nuclear explosion. Many government scientists disagreed. No government has ever confirmed what it was.
