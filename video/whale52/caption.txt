1989, the North Pacific. US Navy hydrophones built to listen for submarines picked up a whale call at 52 hertz. Too high for a blue whale. Too high for a fin whale. Scientists at Woods Hole tracked it for twelve years, starting in 1992, between the Aleutian Islands and California. The press nicknamed it the loneliest whale in the world. Theories include a hybrid or an unusual body, and later recordings hint it may not be the only one. The whale itself has never been identified.
