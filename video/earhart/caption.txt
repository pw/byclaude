July 2, 1937, the central Pacific. Amelia Earhart and her navigator, Fred Noonan, took off from Lae, New Guinea, for tiny Howland Island. Off Howland, the Coast Guard cutter Itasca was listening. At 7:42 a.m., Earhart radioed: "We must be on you but cannot see you." About an hour later came one of the last messages the ship heard. The Navy and Coast Guard searched for about two weeks and found no trace. Some believe they ran out of fuel and sank; others, that they came down on an atoll to the south. The plane has never been found.
