More than 2,000 carved stone jars stand on a plateau in northern Laos, some three metres tall. When archaeologists excavated one smaller jar at another site, they found the bones of at least 37 people, placed there over a span of possibly up to 270 years, around AD 890 to 1160. Who carved the jars, and exactly how they were used, is still argued. Visitors keep to cleared paths; beyond them lie unexploded bombs from the 1964–1973 American air war.
