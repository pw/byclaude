Claims
1. "More than 2,000 carved stone jars ... northern Laos" — UNESCO World Heritage listing 1587, Megalithic Jar Sites in Xiengkhuang (https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1587/; "more than 2,100 tubular-shaped megalithic stone jars"; inscribed 2019); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_of_Jars ("Documented sites now include over 2,100 jars"). Note: jar counts vary by source (300–2,500 estimates), "more than 2,000" is the documented figure.
2. "some three metres tall" — Wikipedia (jars 1–3 m, up to 14 tons); https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/a-discovered-trove-of-bones-and-teeth-yields-new-clues-to-the-century-old-mystery-of-death-jars-in-laos-180988772/ ; https://phys.org/news/2026-04-giant-jars-ancient-bells-bones.html ("up to 3 meters high").
3. "In May 2026 archaeologists opened one and found the bones of at least 37 people" — Antiquity news release 19 May 2026 https://www.antiquity.ac.uk/news/2026/ancient-burial-practices-emerge-laos-mysterious-plain-jars ("remains of at least 37 people"); Smithsonian Magazine 20 May 2026 (link above); Laotian Times https://laotiantimes.com/2026/05/19/remains-of-37-people-discovered-in-giant-stone-jar-in-xieng-khouang/
4. "added over roughly 270 years between the 9th and 12th centuries" — Antiquity release ("possibly up to 270 years ... secondary internment during the 9th and 12th centuries AD"); Smithsonian ("added ... over several hundred years, between the 9th and 12th century C.E."; Wikipedia gives 890–1160 CE). Caption says "roughly".
5. "Who carved the rest, and exactly how they were used, is still argued" — Antiquity release, Skopal: "Archaeologists generally agree they were used in mortuary rituals, but we don't know how they were exactly used, who made them or how old they are"; phys.org/Pursuit (Apr 2026): "who carved them, when they were carved and how they were transported remain a mystery"; Smithsonian: researchers say unclear whether the ritual was common across the Plain or local to that jar. Some "mystery solved" headlines (Bangkok Post, SN Explores) overstate; the authors themselves hedge. Hence NO "Still unsolved." line; status = debated.
6. "Visitors keep to cleared paths; beyond them lie unexploded bombs from the 1964–1973 American air war" — Legacies of War https://legaciesofwar.org/about-laos/secret-war-laos/ (US bombing of Laos 1964–1973; over 270 million cluster munitions, ~30% failed to detonate; UXO contaminates all 17 provinces); Wikipedia Plain_of_Jars (MAG cleared the most visited sites 2004–05, further sites 2007; <10% of megaliths thoroughly investigated; marker bricks); Smithsonian ("Vietnam War left behind dangerous unexploded ordnance and deep craters. Cleanup efforts ... have made accessing the plateau feasible"); https://www.laoslife.info/jar-site-1-plain-of-jars/ (stay on designated paths). Caption deliberately does not quote the 270-million figure (Laos-wide, not Plain-only; Wikipedia's phrasing is looser).

Checked 2024–2026 news: the May 2026 Antiquity excavation (Site 75, ~70 km NE of Phonsavan, Skopal/Bounxayhip, James Cook University + Lao Dept of Heritage) is the only major development; Apr 2026 Univ. Melbourne/Pursuit feature (Shewan, O'Reilly) says the central mystery endures.
