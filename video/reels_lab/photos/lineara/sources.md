- "Minoan palace of Zakros on Crete" / "destroyed around 1450 BC": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakros (New Palace "destroyed around 1450 BC"; 31 Linear A tablets, second largest source after Haghia Triada). Corroborate: Linear A in use c. 1800-1450 BC, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_A (never again used for administration after the LM IB destructions).
- "Scholars can sound out many signs using values borrowed from Linear B... the words match no known language": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_A (approximate values for most syllabic signs inferred from Linear B; underlying language not determined; resulting words uninterpretable). Also https://theconversation.com/cracking-the-code-can-ai-help-us-decipher-ancient-languages-288238 (Linear A = language isolate, no confirmed link to any known language).
- "Linear B deciphered in 1952": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_B (deciphered 1952 by Michael Ventris, building on Alice Kober).
- "About 1,400 Linear A inscriptions survive": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_A ("roughly 1400 inscriptions, amounting to 7400 sign tokens").
- "The numbers can be read": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_A ("Integers can be read ... numerals follow a decimal system").
- "Still unsolved" as of Oct 2026: no accepted decipherment. A June 2026 amateur/AI-assisted claim (Tom Di Mino; 40 signs, 408-word lexicon, Semitic) is unverified and not accepted: The Conversation (above); https://greekreporter.com/2026/07/30/ai-engineer-claim-decipher-linear-a/
