Claims
- "steatite", "about 4,000 years ago", "3.5 centimetres wide": Cleveland record above (technique steatite; c. 2000 BCE; overall 3.5 x 3.6 cm). Find spot not stated by the museum, so the caption does not claim Mohenjo-daro/Harappa.
- "No one can read the signs" / undeciphered: Wikipedia Indus script ("Despite many attempts, the script has not yet been deciphered.") https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_script ; Smithsonian (short inscriptions, no bilingual text, unknown whether signs are words/syllables) https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/officials-are-offering-1-million-to-anyone-who-can-decode-this-ancient-script-180985922/
- "Some 5,000 inscriptions unearthed since 1924": Wikipedia, "In 2025, it was reported around 5,000 inscriptions have been excavated since 1924" (earlier counts: 2,906 legible by 1977, ~4,000 by 1992). Discovery announcement 1924, Marshall, Illustrated London News (worldhistory.org/Indus_Script).
- "most about five signs long": Wikipedia, "The average length of the inscriptions is around five signs"; Smithsonian, "usually about five symbols long".
- "no bilingual text": Wikipedia, "There is no known bilingual inscription to help decipher the script."; Smithsonian (no Rosetta-Stone-like text).
- "$1 million prize announced in 2025": Tamil Nadu CM M.K. Stalin, 5 Jan 2025. https://theprint.in/india/tn-cm-stalin-announces-usd-1-million-prize-for-decoding-indus-valley-script/2432249/ ; https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/cm-stalin-announces-1-million-prize-for-decoding-indus-valley-script-125010500330_1.html ; Smithsonian above.
- "none has been accepted" (as of Oct 2026): searches for 2025-2026 news found no accepted decipherment or prize award. ASI held/announced an international conference "Decipherment of Indus Script: Current status and Way Forward" 20-22 Aug (ThePrint, 20 Jun 2025: meaning "remains a mystery") https://theprint.in/feature/asi-calling-researchers-to-crack-indus-valley-script/2664475/ ; an Aug 2026 BozokMedia feature (403 to fetch, seen only as search snippet) still describes a "global race to decipher". Rival full-decipherment claims (Sanskrit cryptogram preprint, protocuneiform/Dravidian paper) are unaccepted/non-peer-reviewed.
