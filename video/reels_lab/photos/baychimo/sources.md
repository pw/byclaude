# Sources: baychimo

Image: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Baychimo.jpg (Commons API: LicenseShortName "Public domain"; wikitext tag {{PD-Canada}}, "Old photograph (1931)"; 1260x814)

- "In 1931 ... steamer Baychimo ... Hudson's Bay Company": owned by HBC from 1921; trapped in pack ice 1 Oct 1931. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Baychimo ; https://www.thevintagenews.com/2018/09/11/ss-baychimo-ghost-ship/
- "left behind in the Arctic ice off Alaska, after her crew took off the most valuable furs": crew retrieved "the most valuable furs from the hold" then abandoned her (Wikipedia, Abandonment); vintagenews: captain unloaded cargo and valuables, abandoned. Location near Barrow/Point Barrow, Alaska.
- "She did not sink": after the Nov 1931 blizzard she was found afloat ~45 mi away (Wikipedia). 
- "Prospectors, explorers and Inuit reported her drifting for decades": Wikipedia Sightings list: prospectors (1932), Iñupiat boarded March 1933, explorers on a schooner July 1934, Inuit 1962 and 1969. Vintagenews: ~12 sightings over ~40 years.
- "in 1969 she was seen frozen in pack ice": last recorded sighting, by a group of Inuit, stuck fast in pack ice between Point Barrow and Icy Cape (Wikipedia; vintagenews "last recorded sighting ... 1969").
- "Nobody has found her since": Wikipedia "She has not yet been found" (2006 Alaskan government project, nothing found); vintagenews "no wreckage has been found". 2024-2026 check: web search found no report of a discovery; a Jan 2026 Boing Boing piece (403 to fetch; seen in search snippet) still says the wreck is missing: https://boingboing.net/2026/01/16/the-ghost-ship-that-drifted-the-arctic-for-38-years.html
