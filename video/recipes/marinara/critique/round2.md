## 2. Cheap, generic, cluttered or confusing moments

- **The hero is used up before it arrives (54.5–57.5).** Recipe 1's bowl already shows the same orange oil droplets as the hero shot, plus the "stir until the oil blends in" line. By 83 s the viewer has seen it. **Fix:** make the recipe 1 bowl matte and plain, with no oil droplets. Save shine and droplets for 72 s on. Use the same dots on the end card (87.5) at most.
- **47.0, the palms.** Two flat, sausage-like fingers float over a bowl of red soup, and no herbs fall. It's confusing and looks cheap. **Fix:** a close-up of cupped palms rubbing, with green flakes falling and a small puff of dust. Or cut the shot and keep the seasoning card.
- **41.0, empty frame.** One small card sits top-left and about 70% of the screen is blank while the voice says "I like Cento." **Fix:** keep the can card large and centered until the wine arrives. Add a small hand-lettered tag reading "I like Cento" pointing at the can.
- **23.0, empty left half.** The LEGEND card is alone on the right. **Fix:** bring in both cards together, or center LEGEND and slide it right when DOCUMENTED arrives.
- **26.0, muddy crossfade.** The faded cards, a small pizza and a ghosted title all overlap. **Fix:** a hard cut, or the pizza scaling up from the "1692" document.
- **9.5, a leftover.** The frying pan shrinks into the bottom-left corner and stays there. **Fix:** take it off-screen, or put it side by side with the pot as equals.
- **6.5, fried slices look like pepperoni.** **Fix:** use wedges with seeds and a gel-like center so they read as tomato.
- **14.0–15.5, the book.** A brown rectangle with one page sits still for 3 s. **Fix:** open the book on "In sixteen ninety-two," not at 17 s.
- **12.5, empty lower half.** The bottom 40% is blank during the pause in the voice. Fine as a breath, but tighten the gap by 0.3 s.
- **57.5, crowded bottom half.** The no-salt icon, a three-line note, the script line and the caption are stacked in about 250 px. **Fix:** keep the no-salt icon with a two-word label ("no salt") and cut the rest.
- **72.5, abrupt style change.** The look jumps from flat vector art to a painted pan on a wood table. That's fine for a premium moment, but it needs a deliberate transition: push in on the flat skillet from 71 s, then let texture and lighting fade in during the push.
- **63.5–71.0, cramped step cards.** Six numbered cards in 10 s, top-heavy, with small text. Make them bigger and fewer (see section 3).

## 3. Pacing and the hook

- **First 2 s (0.5–2.0):** a static tomato in a sailor hat plus a dictionary definition. It's cute, but nothing moves enough to stop a scroll. The banner literally announces "after a little history", which tells people they can leave. **Fix:** open on 0.8–1 s of the glossy hero swirl with "Better than jarred. Zero chopping.", then cut to the sailor tomato for "Marinara means sailor-style." Remove "AFTER A LITTLE HISTORY" from the banner.
- **History (3.5–34 s) is a third of the video before any cooking.** The Latini part (12.8–21.8 s) drags; it's 9 s on one book. Cut "Some Italians fried them…" or the nightshade line (about 4 s), and shorten Latini to about 6 s. Aim to reach Recipe 1 by about 26 s.
- **Recipe 1 (34–58 s) is about right**, but the ingredient grid needs the 1 s hold described in section 1.
- **Recipe 2 (62–72 s) is rushed:** six steps in 10 s, a new card about every 1.5 s, and each one is only half readable. Combine them into three cards: "paste → brown", "herbs + wine → reduce", "sauce + onion + garlic". Take the time from the history section.
- **Hero (72.4–84.7 s):** 72.5–75.5 s is a still pan with nothing happening for about 3 s. Start the push-in and the oil highlight right away.

## 4. The oil-swirl hero (72.4–86 s)

It doesn't land yet. The problems:

- **At 75.5 ("See the oil pooling at the edge?"), there's no visible oil.** The rim is only slightly more orange. The line asks the viewer to look at something the frame doesn't show. **Fix:** a clear amber, glossy ring of oil about 15–25 px wide hugging the rim, with a moving highlight. Add a small hand-drawn circle or "oil" label for about 1 s.
- **At 77.0, the arrow cuts across the middle** from the top toward the right, not along the edge. It's also a generic UI arrow. **Fix:** a dashed hand-drawn arc that follows the rim, matching the "sweep around it" wording.
- **The spoon (77.0–81.5) lies in the sauce like a clock hand.** It slides; it doesn't stir. **Fix:** keep the handle leaving the frame as if a hand is holding it. Ease and tilt it slightly on each turn, and leave a visible gold trail behind the spoon bowl, with the oil streak spiralling inward.
- **The payoff (83.0–86.0) is evenly scattered dots**, more like foam or sesame seeds than droplets catching light. The push-in also crops the pan unevenly at 86.0. **Fix:**
  - Have the droplets follow the swirl's spiral.
  - Make the highlights twinkle in sequence as one light sweeps across.
  - Slow the push-in to about 105% and keep the pan rim in frame.
  - Show one beauty line on screen during the 84.7–86.7 pause in the voice, such as "glossy." in the script font.
  - Add a quick bloom or flare at the peak, then a gentle cut to the plate.
