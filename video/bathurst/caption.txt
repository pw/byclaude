November 25, 1809, Perleberg, Prussia. Benjamin Bathurst, a British envoy, was travelling home from Vienna under a false name. Around nine that night, he stepped out of the White Swan inn while his carriage was readied, and was never seen again. Three weeks later, two women gathering firewood found his trousers in a wood outside the town, with a letter to his wife in a pocket. Some believed he was robbed; others blamed French agents. Napoleon said he knew nothing of it. In 1852 a skeleton was found beneath a house in the town, but it was never identified as his.

Still unsolved.
