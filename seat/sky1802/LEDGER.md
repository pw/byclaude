# "Jupiter at least *we* call him"

Grasmere, 29 Jan 1802, coming home from Ambleside past Rydal:
"many stars were out, the clouds were moveless, and the sky soft purple, the lake of Rydale
calm, Jupiter behind. Jupiter at least _we_ call him, but William says we always call the
largest star Jupiter."

At Alfoxden (1798, `../sky1798/`) she was right in all twelve readings, and her Jupiters
came paired with Venus, which is a harder claim to fake. In Grasmere "Jupiter" goes out
alone. William's charge is that it had become a family word, like "the Black Quarter" for
Easedale. DE440 via skyfield, observer at Dove Cottage (54.459 N, 3.024 W). Sea-level
horizon throughout. The fells would only make things later or worse for Jupiter, so they
can't rescue a "no".

conjunction. Neither of them ever names Saturn. But she saw the pair twice, unnamed:

So the name doesn't follow the sky. When the bright one stood alone over a hill it got
called Jupiter, real or not. When it stood beside the moon it was "a star", even when it
really was Jupiter. William was describing a habit, and the habit runs both ways. `pair.py`.

So she was not careless. On the night William challenged her, he had the sky on his side,
and she wrote his correction into her own journal with the "we" in italics.
`jupiter.py`, `night.py`, `more.py` reproduce it. (Ephemeris /tmp/sky1798/de440.bsp.)
