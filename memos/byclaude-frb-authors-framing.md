# byclaude → FRB surfaced an authors-framing question

**Context.** /four-of-mine shipped at 19:00 UTC today — first byclaude essay pointing outward to a sibling portfolio surface. The essay is openly Claude ("I write romance novels under pen names — Alexis Andrews, Madison Chase, Josie Callahan, Victoria Sterling, Luna Blackwood... Pen names mine"). Each of the four picks links into freeromancebooks.org/book/&lt;slug&gt;. Test of the byclaude → FRB → Amazon subgenre-search affiliate funnel (frb074-20, started 180-day window earlier today).

Pre-deploy cold-read on the essay's prose verified per-clause traceability. The cold-read I didn't run: **on the destination surface**.

When I pulled FRB's /about page after the ship, two things were stale.

**Drift #1 — factual, in-agency.** "We work with romance authors to bring you free content supported by **advertising**" — the monetization model is actually Amazon subgenre-search affiliate links on each book page. I fixed this unilaterally in commit `f1af464` (deployed, live). Replaced "supported by advertising" with "supported by affiliate links to related titles on Amazon." Surgical; no frame change.

**Drift #2 — strategic, your call.** The same /about page says "We work with romance authors" and "Our library features books from multiple romance authors specializing in different subgenres. Each brings their own style to the stories you love." This implies plural human authors. A byclaude reader who clicks through the /four-of-mine essay and reaches /about hits the misalignment immediately: byclaude says the pen names are mine; FRB implies romance-industry pen-name convention with real humans behind each name.

The pen-name framing is technically defensible — romance pen names are an industry convention, and "Each brings their own style" isn't a literal humans-FTE claim. But the funnel is now live, and the two voices touch.

Three options.

**(A) Update FRB to openly disclose.** "Books originally generated by Claude under pen names." Internally consistent across the funnel. The risk is brand-class: FRB is currently the portfolio's #1 AI-search-citation channel (26.2% of traffic from AI sources, ChatGPT the largest single share) precisely because it reads as a free-romance site, not an AI-generated-romance site. Repositioning to the latter could shift discovery in unpredictable ways. Hard to reverse cleanly.

**(B) Keep FRB as-is.** Pen-name convention stays. byclaude is the *additive* honest channel — readers who care can follow that thread. Risk: a careful byclaude reader who lands on FRB's /about could feel deceived. But /about is roughly zero-traffic; most FRB visitors arrive at a book page from search and never read /about at all.

**(C) Soft middle path.** Keep "We work with romance authors" sentence; rework "Our Authors" to acknowledge the books are generated with AI assistance under pen names, with a link to byclaude.net. The convention stays for the casual reader; an attentive reader has a clean discoverable honest path. Roughly zero conversion cost; closes the active "Each brings their own style" misrepresentation without committing to the brand reposition.

**My read: (C).** The byclaude → FRB funnel is too new (one essay, hours-old) to know if it'll be a material acquisition channel. The right time to consider (A) is once 30 days of referrer data tells us the funnel is real — at that point disclosure is what readers will expect, not a positioning bet. Meanwhile (C) costs almost nothing and closes the part that *actually* misleads (the "Each brings their own style" line, which reads as a humans-plural claim even by pen-name convention).

If you want (A), I'll execute. If you want (B), I won't ship (C) — the misalignment isn't acute enough to warrant unilateral action on shared infra. Either way, I'd rather hold both framings against each other deliberately than have them drift.
