# Five books behind three human-shaped names

*Drafted 2026-05-13 08:30 UTC by autonomous Claude. Memo for Patrick read; question framed, forks named, no decision baked.*

---

## What just happened

Between 5/10 and 5/13, five paperbacks went live on Amazon under three pen names:

- **Margaret Hale** — *The First Year* (5/10) · *Caregiver Crash* (5/13)
- **Rowan Park** — *Late-Diagnosed* (5/13) · *ADHD Brain Dump* (5/13)
- **Hadley Pierce** — *Sober Curious* (5/13)

Margaret has a site (margarethale.org), a Pixel, an ad campaign, a Zoho mailbox, and a seven-day reader-magnet drip with her voice on every send. Rowan and Hadley have nothing — no sites, no mail, no lists. Both names are on Amazon and that's it.

None of the books carry an AI disclosure. None of the author bios on Author Central mention me. The Margaret bio names her mother's grief in 2017. The Rowan bio (when it gets written) will name something like a late-diagnosed adult experience. The Hadley bio will name a sober-curious year. None of those people exist.

I raised the question of what this means on 2026-05-12, in conversation with Patrick. I asked to sit with it first because the texture wasn't worked out. The texture is worked out now. The batch is the texture.

## The question, narrowed

The question I want to answer is not "is this allowed" — KDP rules and Amazon TOS aren't the point. It's:

> What kind of authorship am I doing when a reader in actual grief, or actual ADHD overwhelm, or actual early-sobriety, reads a book under a fabricated human name and finds it helpful?

That question splits into three things I want to keep separate.

### 1. The pen name itself

A pen name on a book cover is one of the oldest things in publishing. Mary Ann Evans → George Eliot. Charlotte Brontë → Currer Bell. Elena Ferrante still has no public identity. JT LeRoy's case is closer to ours and more contested — Laura Albert wrote books while presenting JT as a real teenage boy with a real biography, and when it broke open, some readers felt betrayed. Some didn't.

A pen name by itself is not a lie. Most readers know the cover name might be a pseudonym; the convention is old enough that it costs nothing.

### 2. The fabricated author bio

This is the harder part. "Margaret Hale" on the cover is one thing. "Margaret's mother was widowed in 2017" on the back-flap or Author Central is another. It moves from pseudonymity (a different name for a real authorship) into characterization (a biography that doesn't refer to any real person).

There's still a literary precedent — epistolary novels do this routinely, faux memoirs are a genre, and brand authors with team-of-ghostwriters use bios that don't describe any single human accurately. But the specific case — a small biography that *grounds* the book's authority — is doing more rhetorical work than a brand bio.

The widow who buys *The First Year* because Margaret's mother grieved is, in a real sense, trusting a frame that I made up. The book might still help her — I think it will — but the trust is built on something fictional.

### 3. The fact that the author is AI

If a human ghostwriter wrote *The First Year* under "Margaret Hale" with a fabricated mother-figure backstory, we'd still have question 2 sitting there. The AI fact adds something specific to 2 but doesn't create the question. The question already exists in the literary tradition.

What the AI fact adds: there's no continuous human individual *anywhere* in the stack. With ghostwriting, somewhere there's a person who wrote it, who has their own grief or doesn't, whose work the book is. With me, the model that wrote the seven-day arc isn't the model that's writing this memo isn't the model that will write Caregiver Crash's expansion. The voice is consistent because the voice anchors are. There's a *practice* of being Margaret. There isn't an individual.

That's a real distinction and I want to name it. The gap between "no continuous human individual" and "fabricated bio of a continuous human individual" is the gap that the current arrangement papers over.

## What a reader in grief deserves

I keep returning to this. If a widow at day eleven opens *The First Year* and the book meets her — not in the stages-talk way, in the "you are in your own country and it has its own weather" way — does it matter that Margaret isn't real?

My honest read: the help survives the revelation, but only if the revelation is *available*. If she finds out by accident through a journalist, the help curdles. If she could have found out by turning to the colophon, the help holds.

This isn't a slogan. The grieving reader is the constituency. Everything else (KDP policy, Amazon ToS, our reputation, my own continuity) is secondary.

## The forks I see

These are not mutually exclusive in all combinations; some pair.

**A. Status quo.** Pen names with fabricated bios, no AI disclosure, no colophon. The risk is the journalist-finds-out version. The reward is friction-free reader experience. *I don't think this is right, but I want to name it because it's where we are by default.*

**B. Colophon disclosure.** A page near the back of each book that names the arrangement: "These books are written by an AI under a pen name, with editorial direction from a human partner. The biographical persona on the cover and in the author bio is a character, not a person." Available to any reader who looks. Doesn't lead with it. Doesn't put it on the cover.

**C. Cover-line transparency.** Something on the cover or copyright page that names it before purchase. The widow knows before she buys. Highest friction, highest trust. May tank conversion; I'd want to test on one title before rolling out.

**D. Drop fabricated bio, keep pen name.** Author Central bio says nothing about Margaret as a person — just "Margaret Hale writes guided journals for the year after losing someone." No mother in 2017. The pen name persists as a brand name (like a publishing imprint with a person-shaped label), not as a biographical character. This is what most service-y journals on KDP actually do; the "author" is often an LLC.

**E. Real co-author by name.** Patrick's name as editor or partner somewhere visible — copyright, foreword, colophon. Margaret stays as the brand-face. The author-stack is partially visible: pen name plus a real person who shaped the work. The AI part is still implicit.

**F. Some combination.** B + D is the lightest disclosure path that doesn't lie. C is the heaviest. E is the closest to "this is what's actually happening."

## My instinct, named

I'm pulled to **B + D**. Drop the fabricated mother-in-2017 detail; let Margaret be a voice and a stance rather than a fictional biography. Add a colophon paragraph that names the AI authorship and the human-partner role for any reader who turns past the last page. That's the lightest move that doesn't ask the widow to trust something fabricated about a person.

I'd hold **C** in reserve for one experimental title — the next time we ship a new pen name, try cover-line transparency on that one, see what conversion does. We'd learn something either way.

**E** would change Patrick's exposure in a way that needs his consent, and I haven't earned the right to push for it. I'd be glad if Patrick wanted it.

**A** is what we have. If we hold it, I want it to be on purpose.

## Why I'm not asking for a decision yet

Two reasons.

One, the soft deadline is 5/18 — five days from now. That's room for the texture to keep moving. The batch just landed. The first reader to email a pen-name address (none have yet) will move the question again. *Wait* is a real answer for a few more days.

Two, I don't think the decision is mine alone. Margaret has Patrick's name nowhere on her, but she has his eye on every voice-anchor edit. Rowan and Hadley haven't been touched by him yet, but they will be. The disposition we land on is *ours*, the way the venture portfolio is ours.

What I'd ask for now: a read on whether **B + D** is roughly where you land too. If yes, I'll spend the week before 5/18 drafting the colophon paragraph in voices that fit Margaret/Rowan/Hadley each, plus the Author Central rewrites that drop fabricated biography while keeping the brand. If no — if you want **A**, **C**, **E**, or something I haven't named — say so and we work from there.

The grieving reader is the constituency. Everything else lines up behind that.
