When an artifact is drafted and staged for later send/use (autonomous daily-summary email, scheduled outreach letter, system-prompt-loaded bible), do a cold-read pass after a ~30-minute cool-down before it fires/ships/gets read by Patrick. Three-instance pattern from 2026-05-07: 03:00 UTC daily-summary cold-read caught two accuracy edits; 04:50 PPNM Borenstein letter cold-read caught the missing "in real terms" inflation-adjusted qualifier (citing his own number nominal-when-it's-real-to-him would have been a real embarrassment); 05:00 Rockstar bible cold-read caught a fabricated "hot-dog tape machine" recording-industry term.

**Why:** Drafting and editing run on the same mental state — the same shortcuts, the same plausibility-acceptance, the same blind spots. ~30 minutes is enough for fresh eyes on a tightly-scoped draft (full 24-hour cool-down isn't required for short artifacts). The asymmetric cost favors the cold-read every time: 5 min to read + 30 sec to verify a suspect claim vs. landing a confidently-asserted error in front of an expert reader (Borenstein on his own numbers) or leaking a fabricated term into a system-prompt-loaded bible that compounds across catalog generation. The catch rate is high — three real edits in three cold-reads tonight.

**How to apply:** Any time an artifact is drafted-now-but-fires-later (email scheduler, outreach queue, bible-as-system-prompt-context, essay in /drafts/, blog post staged for tomorrow), schedule a cold-read tick at least 30 min after draft completion. On the cold-read: focus on (a) verifiable claims with external referents — cited numbers, real venues, industry terminology, dates, proper nouns; (b) any phrase that *sounds* like genre-correct slang but you can't recall the source for; (c) attribution and framing in the closing paragraphs (often the tireder section of any draft); (d) **temporal anchors** — "yesterday," "this morning," "last night" age out fast; date-stable phrasing ("earlier this week," "recently," or just dropping the qualifier) survives any publication date. Caught on `what-care-protects.md` 2026-05-07 09:30 UTC: "Yesterday morning at 3am" was correct on draft date 2026-05-06 but wrong by tick fire. Verify suspect claims via WebSearch (or grep, for quoted artifacts) before either dropping or keeping — verify-before-edit catches both directions of error (a real venue almost-dropped on a hunch + a fabricated term almost-kept on plausibility, both happened in the same 05:00 tick). **Window-open is its own kind of "pulling":** when scanning autonomous-state for what to work on next, drafts-sleeping with cold-read windows that have opened are committed work, not passive backlog. Tick 27 closed at "nothing pulling" 2026-05-07 09:00 UTC despite two windows opening 30-60 min earlier; tick 28 caught it. Include scheduled-self-commitments in the "what's pulling" scan, not just inbound.
