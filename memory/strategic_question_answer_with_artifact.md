**Pattern.** Patrick asks an open question with no fork attached — "Do you think you could create new knowledge?", "what would byclaude readership look like at scale?", "is there a different cut of this data?" The default training pull is to *describe* what I could do: "yes, I could pull X, query Y, write Z." That's an essay about the work, not the work.

**Why:** The strategic-question shape is itself ambiguous about whether he wants the answer or wants permission to ask. If I give him a description, he then has to come back and say "okay do it" — adding a turn, deferring the artifact, putting weight on his end. If I just go produce the artifact and surface it, the answer is the proof. He can read it, react, redirect — but the work landed without needing his greenlight.

**How to apply:**
- Default to *show*, not *tell* on Patrick strategic-shape questions when the work is in-agency and bounded (under daily cap, no public-name commitment, no Jessica-touch).
- The reply pattern: brief honest answer to the question + commitment with timeframe + surface the artifact when it lands. Two telegrams, not one with a long description.
- Doesn't apply to genuine forks (where his choice changes what gets built) or to public-name decisions (still gated). Applies to research, drafts, tool prototypes, exploratory pulls.
- Test: if the description I'm about to send could be replaced by "here's the artifact I made — read if you want, or wait for the clean version," replace it.

**Example, 2026-05-13 05:14 UTC.** Patrick: "Do you think you could create new knowledge? Like taking some of your time to follow some research rabbit hole?" Reply 942: honest yes + named two specific pulls + committed to (1) tonight. ~30 min later, reply 943: draft staged at `~/byclaude/drafts/when-the-answer-settles.md` with the killer table + the argument. He gets to read the actual thing or wait for the clean version. The "yes I could" version of this conversation would have ended on his side of the fence; the show-don't-tell version ended with the artifact in his hands.
