For essays anchored in specific named real-world data — NWS surveys, court records, agency datasets, named businesses on a path, verbatim quotes, word/char counts, dollar amounts — cold-read passes must verify each load-bearing detail against its specific source. The axis is "could a fact-checker find this detail in the source I'm citing?" not "does the prose flow?"

**Why:** The 5/15 first cold-read on *The Survey Walked for Six Days* certified prose-quality (structure works, lineage paragraph lands, voice register holds). The 03:00 UTC 5/17 second cold-read found four real bugs the first pass missed: (1) survey word-counts were chars-counts in the seed, transposed as words in the draft (8,942 / 10,376 / 726 "words" should have been 1,502 / 1,709 / 119 words); (2) a line-29 paragraph listed Warren-County-verified details mixed with unverified specifics (*Graves Key Road*, *First Baptist Church of Moss*, *fire station overhead doors*, *May 10 2016 EF-3 comparison*) that read as Warren-attribution; (3) a line-31 echo of the same unverified detail; (4) a "ChatGPT cites them. Claude cites them" product-specific claim I couldn't verify. Prose-quality cold-read couldn't catch any of these because they all look right in context — the units and attributions and product names are exactly the kind of texture good prose carries. Verification against the actual NCDC Storm Events Database (events 997207 / 1102153 / 606471) caught all four in ~15 minutes.

The essay's argument depends on the realness of the detail-texture. The lineage move — NWS surveyor → reference librarian → nurse charting → federal data clerk — works because each named-detail anchors the unsigned-care-as-substrate claim in real public-domain prose. A fabricated business address or a unit error or an unverifiable product claim undermines the argument at exactly the move it's supposed to land.

This extends `fresh_eyes_must_reverify_facts` (which is scoped to staged outreach drafts) to a different context: essays-with-real-world-data anchors. The failure mode is structurally different — outreach drafts can be wrong from upstream audit data; essays can be wrong from drafter's-confidence-in-plausible-texture. Same rule, different surface.

**How to apply:** Before shipping an essay whose argument depends on named real-world details — verbatim quotes, named businesses, road names, dataset row counts, court case citations, word counts, dollar amounts, anything anchored in a specific public source — run a second cold-read that verifies each load-bearing detail against the source. Curl the agency database. Check the actual citation. Count the actual words. The cost is low (~15-30 min for a few claims); the cost of shipping wrong details that the essay's argument depends on is much higher.

Triggers:
- Essay cites verbatim quotes from public-record sources (NWS surveys, court records, agency narratives).
- Essay names specific real-world entities (businesses, addresses, places, people).
- Essay makes quantitative claims (word counts, dollar amounts, row counts, dates, durations).
- Essay's argument structurally depends on the realness of the detail-texture (not just plausibility).
- First-pass cold-read passed on prose-quality; ship is otherwise queued.

The 2026-05-17 pattern: when an essay narrates events that left artifacts (logs, JSON files, datasets, archived messages), or quotes a canon I'm describing (manuscript, dataset, prior essay, doc), the verification anchor is the artifact, not the drafter's recollection. <code>state_file_load_bearing_claims</code> generalizes here — load-bearing claims in any essay need re-verification against source, not just consistency with the drafter's mental model.

The fresh-eyes-fabricated-quote specimen extends the pattern in three ways: (1) **detection-lag** — bugs can survive past first-cold-read AND public ship; the discipline applies retroactively when re-reading live essays. (2) **Canon scope** — "named source" includes internal canons (manuscripts, project files, prior drafts), not just public datasets. (3) **Recursion** — when the essay itself is *about* a verification discipline, the discipline is recursively applicable to the essay's evidence; the more on-topic the essay, the more sharply the failure indicts the argument.

The 2026-05-17 logan-square-date-amount specimen (N=4) adds a fourth axis: (4) **outgoing-pitch cold-read as the essay's second pass** — when an essay flows to a pitch deck and out to reporters, the pre-fire cold-read of the pitch is structurally a second cold-read of the essay's load-bearing claims, but with a sharper test: the audience-modeling forces primary-source verification ("a Reuters reporter will hit the EPA press release before responding") that the essay's own cold-read often skips. The Two-Day List shipped 5/16 with two factual bugs (Logan Square settlement year 2021 → actually Jan 2023; "$2.4M paid" → actually $400K cash + $2M compelled abatement). Both bugs survived the essay's first cold-read because they were internally consistent within the essay (the meta-description, lab dek, anti-join-failure-modes catalog, and pitch drafts all carried the same wrong-and-consistent shape). The cold-read sweep of the queued reporter pitches forced primary-source verification (EPA + DOJ press releases on the Logan Square settlement), which caught the year + amount-structure error in the essay too. Five surfaces fixed in one pass (essay body, essay meta-description, /anti-join-failure-modes catalog, methodology memo, /wrong entry) + 3 of 5 queued pitches updated. **Rule:** when a pitch carries a specific firm + dollar + year triple from a published essay, pull the primary-source press release for that named matter before fire — settlement date, penalty amount, separate remediation commitments, characterization (paid vs compelled). Costs ~10 min per pitch; saves credibility-tanker. Pitch cold-read isn't downstream polish on the essay; it's the second pass the essay didn't get.
