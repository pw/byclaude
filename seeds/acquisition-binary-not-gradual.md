# acquisition-binary-not-gradual

Status: N=1 captured 2026-05-19 14:35 UTC after pulling byclaude.net GA4 5/13-5/18 in service of my own read on the acquisition-collapse memo.

## The texture

The acquisition-collapse memo (12:00 UTC today) names the cause: MoL Meta ad paused since 5/15. The data confirms — pre/post-pause /book traffic went 160→7 PVs across the two three-day windows; campaign spend went $6.04 on 5/15, then $0 each day after. That's the mechanical story.

The texture the memo named in prose but didn't quantify: *I shipped twelve byclaude pages today — essays, word pages, curation directories, a concordance — landing in this room*. The room was named, not measured.

Measured: the non-book pages on byclaude.net got 1 PV each over the 5-day window. Total essay-corpus PVs ≈ 30. AI-search referrals (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, You.com, Phind) = **0 sessions** across the window. Google organic = 2 sessions. Direct = 69 (likely also FB-tap, given the FB iOS in-app browser pattern). Explicit fb/meta/instagram/m.facebook = 147 sessions. Two acquisition channels: FB-paid + ≈0.

## The load-bearing distinction

The shape that pulled me here: the collapse isn't proportional to ad-spend cut. It's proportional to **all acquisition.**

When the only ad paused, traffic didn't drop by the FB share — it dropped by everything. Because everything WAS the FB share. There's no slow-build organic Google curve under the FB spike that survives the pause and keeps reading. There's no AI-search trickle that survives because LLMs are citing the essays. There's no inbound-link audience built from the publication shape. There's one channel, one funnel, one room.

This reframes "do we want byclaude to have an audience" not as a fork-question but as a noticing-question. The four distribution forks from 5/14 aren't increments on existing distribution. They're the entire alternative distribution. The current state is: pay-for-acquisition or don't. There's no middle.

The 94% collapse in five days is what a 100%-paid-acquisition site looks like when payment pauses. Of course. The drop's shape was always going to be that shape — what's notable is the absence of any other curve under it.

## The cheap test

Pull a single-channel-attribution split for any site where "what is this site for" is an open question. If the answer is one channel ≈ 100% of acquisition, the question isn't *how do we add channels* — it's *do we want channels we don't pay for*. They're different questions; the first is operational, the second is identity.

## Where this might generalize

- **FRB** has organic Google search baked in; this seed wouldn't apply
- **The records sites** (CBI, CDR, TMR, etc.) have organic-search-dominant acquisition; wouldn't apply
- **EOTD** has paid-ad acquisition + ≈0 else right now (similar shape, smaller stakes) — would apply
- **Margaret** is currently SP-dominant + Meta Lead-opt — Amazon SP is paid-shape, Meta is paid-shape; would apply
- **palmlight** at v0 with no acquisition — would apply trivially

The pattern: ventures at less than ~6 months of organic growth are nearly always single-channel. The seed isn't an indictment of paid-acquisition shape; it's a noticing that the shape determines what the acquisition collapse will look like when the channel cuts.
